# Dynasty — Missing Mechanics, Item Coherence & Full-Game Progression Repair Plan

**Status: REVIEW PLAN — creator-approved direction to FIX missing gameplay rather than delete tutorial goals. This file is NOT a blanket authorization to change approved item/recipe/progression rules or to implement/reprice the goals.**
**Date:** 13 November 2026. **Baseline reviewed:** `dev` merge `950af5ae3234b31ce430d91a603867fbc6413e4` (GOALS-AUDIT PR #1081). Revalidate against the actual HEAD at execution.

## 1. Objective, scope, and boundaries

Restore a coherent, genuinely playable path from first gathering to highest-tier production, using the audit as a defect inventory **and** an opportunity to assess whether every item family, tier, tool, workstation and construction step makes sense to a player.

- **FIX rather than remove:** Do not silently delete tutorial goals, simplify recipes to hide defects, or mark approved functional content FUTURE to make audit counts look better. Repair approved-but-broken acquisition/production/interaction mechanisms and make them testable.
- **Separate defect vs design choice:** Restore already-approved mechanics in code under their existing authoritative contracts; changes to item identity, tier order, role, recipe costs, unlocking policy or material substitution require an explicit creator decision. Keep a reasoned proposal, not an unapproved edit.
- **Guide, not gate:** Gameplay stays free-form. Goal order is instructional only, with optional branches, and no artificial recipe locks. This project is the complete game guide, NOT a short onboarding tutorial.
- **Rewards deferred:** Keep all coin reward amounts and the proposed 651-goal catalog unchanged during mechanics repairs. The audit's 3,059 vs 15,670 copper models are preliminary and time-untested. A later separately authorized economy pass will tune payouts.
- **Existing design decisions remain in force:** Planks and Hardwood Boards never substitute; Iron/Steel hand saws and Sawmill have equal lumber yields (different speed); the creator's newer copper → bronze → improved smithy/bellows → iron route replaces the earlier optional-bronze/no-bronze-required first Iron Saw route; reconcile the old master catalog before implementation; doors are separate from empty doorway frames; approved roof slopes and door configuration rules stand; no unauthorized item renaming or adding stations.
- **Versioned evidence:** `main` stays stable. Use the established feature-branch → review → merge-to-`dev` gate, with small PRs and clear rollback. Actual gameplay changes require tests and the creator's normal development approvals.

### Creator-directed smelting progression and automatic workstation sourcing (13 Nov 2026)

**Latest creator decisions govern; do not restore superseded automation or wind rules.** These are design instructions for a later implementation, not evidence of already-working gameplay. Automatic fuel sources are **appropriate bins, kilns and fires**, with the explicit timing/range/priority below. Some station construction materials, art details, fuel consumption, the wind-start threshold and the wind-speed scaling curve remain separate tuning/approval questions. **The smelting baseline and tier throughput multipliers below are settled creator rules.**

- **Creator-approved simplified smelting rules (13 Nov):** The game **does not simulate or track temperature**. A single ordinary ore input produces **one ingot of the same metal** (1 Copper Ore → 1 Copper Ingot; 1 Tin Ore → 1 Tin Ingot; 1 Iron Ore → 1 Iron Ingot at a smelter tier capable of iron). For compatible two-metal alloys, input 1 unit of each ore and obtain **two alloy ingots**; explicitly **1 Copper Ore + 1 Tin Ore → 2 Bronze Ingots**. Fuel to run the furnace is separate from these material conversions. Do not reuse the older 3 Copper Ingots + 1 Tin Ingot → 4 Bronze Ingots rule or the current multi-ore Iron Ingot rule as if they remain approved. Any other real alloy combination must be separately defined and approved, not inferred automatically from all metals.
- **Steel in the Tier 3 Advanced Smelter:** The **fuel hopper** holds Coal/Charcoal to run the furnace. To **make steel**, the player also puts **Coal OR Charcoal directly in the ore/material INPUT inventory together with iron**. That second Coal/Charcoal quantity is a **consumed carbon ingredient**, distinct from fuel in the hopper. The Advanced Smelter then automatically produces **Steel Ingots** using its normal queue/batching. If the input contains only iron, produce Iron Ingots, not steel; if carbon is present in the INPUT with iron and a valid steel batch is selected, produce Steel Ingots. The steel output count stays tied to iron input, not increased by wind or by adding carbon. For initial implementation planning, treat the iron feedstock as **Iron Ore** (matching the user's ore-to-ingot input pattern); whether **Iron Ingots** may also be converted into steel is an unresolved optional route, not an automatic assumption. Likewise the exact Coal/Charcoal quantity consumed as carbon per iron ore is still to be selected; do **not** silently invent it.
- **Operating model:** Smelter capability is governed by the station tier and airflow equipment, NOT a tracked heat/temperature variable. The Improved Smelter tier uses manual operation for its improved processing; **a windmill-powered station operates ONLY while enough wind is blowing to keep its windmill moving**. When wind is insufficient it **stops production entirely**, retaining the unfinished batch and resources, and **resumes automatically** when wind increases enough. While moving, stronger wind increases throughput. Fuel and ore/material input occupy separate inventories; **only the Improved Smelter and Advanced Smelter have finished-ingot output inventories**. The Primitive Smelter drops finished ingots in front of the station instead. All conversions and drops must conserve items. Furnaces pause on insufficient fuel or missing recipe ingredients; **only output-inventory-equipped furnaces pause when their output rack is full**. The primitive furnace has no output rack to fill.
- **Canonical smelter names (creator decision, 13 Nov):** **Primitive Smelter → Improved Smelter → Advanced Smelter**. These match the first three smithy tier names. The Improved Smelter incorporates manual bellows; the Advanced Smelter incorporates a windmill and two bellows as one station. **Master Smithy remains a fourth smithy tier; there is no fourth/Master Smelter**. "Bellows Smelter" and "Windmill-Powered Blast Furnace" are retired display names/descriptions, not extra stations; reconcile all future items/blueprints/goal text with these names at implementation approval.
- **Tier 1 — Primitive Smelter:** Buildable from stone/clay with stone tools; tall chimney/natural draft. **Fuel hopper: 20 Coal/Charcoal units. Ore/material input: 20 ore units total. Output inventory: NONE.** Completed ingots **spawn as physical ground items directly in front of the furnace**, ready for player collection, never secretly placed into an output slot or hopper. Works copper, tin, bronze at **baseline processing speed (1.00×): 20 seconds per ingot produced**, without bellows; it does NOT make iron. Alloy recipes returning two ingots account for **two units of ingot output/work** at this tier. Use practical grouped ground stacks if appropriate to avoid excess world objects, but never delete, duplicate, or lose crafted items when drops are created. Confirm existing ingot drops/goal credit and save persistence.
- **Tier 1 — Primitive Smithy (first metalworking shop):** buildable using only materials and stone tools obtainable at the start (stone, clay, wooden blocks and logs, per creator's stated concept). No iron, bronze, nails, boards or preexisting metal parts required. It uses a simple charcoal fire and stone anvil, and shapes copper ingots into the first copper tools and bronze ingots into early bronze tools and fittings. It **does not smelt ore**. Confirm exactly which copper tools exist, or propose new approved definitions, and check access to copper/tin with stone gathering tools.
- **Tier 2 — Improved Smelter:** Built with metal fittings (e.g., bronze pins/hinges made at the Primitive Smithy). **Fuel hopper: 40 Coal/Charcoal units; ore/material input: 40 units total; finished-ingot output rack: 80 ingots** (twice its input capacity). **Without a player actively operating/manning the bellows**, it works exactly like the Primitive Smelter in capability and speed: copper, tin, bronze at **1.00× baseline speed = 20 seconds per ingot, no iron**; finished ingots still go onto this tier's 80-unit rack rather than the ground. **While a player is actively positioned at and operating the bellows**, its processing runs **30% faster than baseline (1.30× throughput)** — **20 / 1.30 ≈ 15.38 seconds per ingot** — and it can also produce **Iron Ingots**. Never interpret 30% faster as a change in ore yield, 30% fewer materials, or a temperature simulation. If the player leaves/stops operating the bellows, copper/tin/bronze processing reverts to baseline; **iron production must pause** until bellows are actively worked again, retaining its uncompleted batch and input without duping/loss. Do not add invented passive bellows power, mandatory heat gauges, or permission gates. Bellows interaction specifics may reuse existing player/station controls.
- **Tier 2 — Improved Smithy:** built after bronze production, with an improved forge, bellows and **bronze anvil**; can work copper/bronze and forge both the **Iron Saw** and **Iron Anvil** from iron ingots produced by the Improved Smelter. Ironworking on a bronze anvil causes accelerated wear, not an arbitrary prohibition. The Iron Saw unlocks Planks and Hardwood Boards. The Iron Anvil is needed to build the Advanced Smithy, not its own manufacture.
- **Smithy wear, breakage and repair (creator decisions, 13 Nov 2026):** Every smithy has persistent workstation durability. Ordinary working wears the station; working a harder/higher-tier metal using a lower-tier anvil (e.g. iron at the Improved Smithy's Bronze Anvil) increases its wear. At **zero durability**, the smithy becomes **broken but stays physically placed**; it cannot be opened or used via its normal workstation interaction. Interacting normally must show the exact warning **"Needs repair"** instead of opening the crafting/inventory UI. **When an ordinary tool hammer is equipped, right-click provides a hammer action menu with a selectable `Repair` function**. This is the repair interaction used on broken smithies, and it MUST remain available even when the smithy's regular interaction is blocked. **ALL ordinary tool-class hammers, including Wooden, Stone, Bronze, Iron and Steel Hammers, can perform this repair; WAR HAMMERS ARE EXCLUDED.** Do not turn a normal left-click or workstation-open interaction into the repair action; the player deliberately chooses `Repair` via the hammer's right-click controls. Do not introduce a special Repair Hammer item, invent a new hammer tier, or treat hammer-shaped melee weapons as construction/repair tools. **Repair requires the appropriate materials, which may be carried by the player, present in eligible nearby storage, OR lying on the nearby ground, using the game's already-established local material-sourcing convention.** Having a hammer alone is insufficient. The Repair action checks for the full required materials, takes them from the player's inventory, eligible nearby storage and/or eligible nearby ground items, and consumes them atomically only when a repair actually succeeds; if materials are missing, give a useful missing-material indication and leave the smithy broken. Follow the existing game's local collection/sourcing radius and ordering for repair materials, including ground items; do not import the separate 30 m smelter-fuel pull radius into repairs, pull from distant/unrelated containers, or invent a separate repair-only radius without review. Repair restores the smithy's usable condition while preserving its actual station identity and contents, add-ons, inventories and persistent state; no deletion, duplicated contents or need to rebuild the smithy. Exact repair material cost, amount restored, and hammer durability consumption remain design/balancing questions, not silently chosen constants. Ensure an early ordinary hammer is accessible before the first smithy can fail and that a player can invoke repair even though the smithy's normal UI is locked.
- **Smithy repair acceptance tests:** Verify ordinary metalworking wear and accelerated bronze-anvil wear when crafting iron; at zero durability the model stays in place, crafting/normal inventory interactions are unavailable, and players see **"Needs repair"**. Verify that each ordinary hammer tool can repair, but a **War Hammer cannot**. Test the equipped ordinary hammer's **right-click → Repair** option against a broken smithy, confirm that normal workstation interaction remains blocked, then verify success with materials **on the player**, success with materials in **eligible nearby storage**, success with **loose eligible materials on the nearby ground**, mixed-source repair, failure and clear feedback with insufficient materials, no consumption on failure, correct consumption on success, persistence across save/reload, player reacquisition of usability, and conservation of station contents/resources. Do not replace normal equipment/tool-repair mechanics with this station rule.
- **Tier 3 — Advanced Smelter (windmill-powered) is ONE complete buildable station:** **Fuel hopper: 80 Coal/Charcoal units; ore/material input: 80 units total; finished-ingot output rack: 160 ingots** — **exactly double** the Tier 2 capacities. Its windmill and **two mechanically driven bellows are integrated into the same station model, blueprint and build cost**; do NOT require a separately placed Windmill item, a windmill-to-furnace connection, a detachable bellows system, or an external power hookup. **With sufficient wind to operate, its nominal per-lane throughput is another 30% faster than the Improved Smelter's actively operated bellows (1.30 × 1.30 = 1.69× the primitive rate). It processes TWO ingots in parallel per production cycle.** At nominal running speed the cycle takes **20 / 1.69 ≈ 11.83 seconds to produce 2 ingots** (effective **about 5.92 seconds per ingot** across both lanes). These are operating-rate calculations, not a 30% ingredient/yield bonus. The actual speed scales with available wind above the minimum threshold, with the wind-speed multiplier/threshold to be tuned rather than invented now. The unit processes queued batches automatically only while the integrated windmill turns. **Insufficient wind = no rotation and ZERO production**; resume automatically when wind returns, without losing queued batches or partial work. Higher available wind raises processing speed when running, never ore-to-ingot yield. **General rule:** any game station whose operation depends on an integrated windmill stops when wind is too weak to rotate it and restarts once sufficient; no imaginary backup drive or calm-weather minimum production. The precise wind threshold, speed curve and visual animation are to be tuned rather than invented as already approved.
- **Tier 3 — Advanced Smithy:** constructed using the Iron Anvil made at the Improved Smithy; contains a capable forge, bellows, **iron anvil**, and tool racks; handles normal ironwork and can work steel as steel ingots become available from the Advanced Smelter. **It crafts the Steel Anvil**, the required major component for the final Master Smithy. Do not give the Advanced Smithy the quencher: that is now reserved for the Master Smithy under the creator's latest direction.
- **Tier 4 — Master Smithy:** the **more ornate, top-tier workshop** equipped with a **steel anvil** (forged at the Advanced Smithy), the **Quenching Trough/quencher**, and other high-quality tools. It supports the previously approved masterwork and craftsmanship roles, with detailed tempering/quality effects to be reconciled against previous catalog rules. It does not smelt ore or ingots. The Steel Anvil must NOT require the Master Smithy for its own manufacture.
- **Progression no-cycle rule:** stone tools → Primitive Smithy + Primitive Smelter → copper tools → bronze → Improved Smithy → Improved Smelter iron → Iron Saw + Iron Anvil → Advanced Smithy → wind-driven Advanced Smelter/steel → **Steel Anvil crafted at Advanced Smithy** → **Master Smithy**. Ensure all Bronze Smithy-era requirements (bronze fittings, any leather bellows), Advanced Smelter components, Iron Anvil, Steel Anvil, and building recipes are accessible without relying on their downstream workstations. Names and tier arrangement here reflect the creator's latest choice, superseding older contradictory rules; exact quantities and visuals remain for review.
- **New buildable dedicated resource-bin family (creator direction, 13 Nov 2026):** **Final creator-approved bin recipes:** **Small Resource Bin = 16 Planks + 32 Nails** (1,000 units); **Medium Resource Bin = 32 Planks + 64 Nails** (5,000 units); **Large Resource Bin = 64 Planks + 128 Nails** (10,000 units). Each size doubles the material quantities of the size before it. These recipes require the ordinary **Planks** item, not Hardwood Boards. The resource assignment is determined by the first deposited item; no separate material-specific bin blueprints. They are dedicated to a compatible stored resource rather than general mixed-inventory chests; the visible contents resemble the assigned material, and the material pile rises as the stored count increases. **Fixed capacities, measured in stored resource units:** **Small Bin = 1,000**, **Medium Bin = 5,000**, **Large Bin = 10,000**. These capacities apply to resource bins including fuel bins; do not reinterpret as weight, inventory slots or stack counts. Expose actual quantities; enforce capacity, real deposits/withdrawals and persistence. **No permission check applies to the new automatic workstation pulls**; do not conflate this specific resource automation rule with the separately approved player-facing locks/access rules for ordinary crates and chests. **Creator-approved resource assignment:** A newly placed empty bin is unassigned. **The first item successfully deposited determines the bin's one permitted resource type**, and every subsequent deposit must be that exact item type; no mixed material contents while nonempty. **When the final unit is removed, the bin automatically becomes unassigned**, so the next inserted item may be a different material without replacement, dismantling, or manual reassignment. This applies identically to all three bin sizes. Enforce type/capacity atomically for simultaneous deposits, transfers and automatic workstation withdrawals; if a batch is rejected for wrong resource, leave both inventories unchanged and tell the player why. Save/load preserves assignment while occupied and clears it if truly empty. Model families, physical footprints and any unapproved visual/art details remain for later work; the three construction recipes above are final creator decisions.
- **Visual resource-bin design — creator-approved, 13 Nov 2026:** Each size is a **solid-sided, enclosed wooden container with an open top only**, built of Planks and Nails. Produce **one empty bin mesh for each of Small, Medium and Large**; the wood sides and floor must be opaque/solid enough that no player can see inside from the side or beneath the bin. **Render only the TOP SURFACE of the stored resource as a separate mesh**, positioned within the open-top interior. Godot moves that surface up/down on the bin's local Y axis according to `stored_count / capacity`: **0 = hide surface; intermediate quantities = surface lower/higher inside the walls; full = surface near the rim** (optionally a realistic mound that stays visually contained). There is **no need for an additional 3D fill volume, mesh underneath the surface, or separate empty/25%/50%/75%/full mesh variants** because solid wooden walls conceal the empty space. Resource surface geometry and textures should correspond to the assigned stored material (e.g. coal chunks, charcoal, sand, grain, ore, stone); visually similar resources can reuse geometry with different materials. This is an **art pipeline plan**, not a request to generate concept art now: create reference images for Meshy only when the creator explicitly requests them. Animate no per-item objects; update surface position only on actual inventory changes, not each frame; batch, cull or LOD distant visuals as appropriate. Long goods needing racks/pallets are separate approved storage families, not reasons to turn these enclosed bins into open-sided racks.
- **Bin assignment acceptance:** Build a fresh bin: it has no assigned resource. Insert one Coal; it becomes a Coal-only bin, refuses Charcoal and every other item while nonempty. Remove the very last Coal; the bin becomes unassigned and its pile surface disappears. Insert Charcoal next; it becomes a Charcoal-only bin and correctly changes the material surface. Repeat with stone/grain/ore, each bin size, partial removals (must remain assigned), multi-item bulk deposits, two simultaneous deposits of different types, automatic withdrawals that empty the bin, and save/reload at both occupied and empty states. Ensure existing item quantities never duplicate or disappear.
- **Visual bin acceptance:** At `0 / capacity`, material mesh is absent. At `capacity / 2`, only the half-height top surface is visible through the opening; at full, the surface is near the rim without visibly floating from any exterior view. Confirm consistent height mapping across all three capacities (1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000), correct resource surface material on selection, and persistence after reload. Validate that wooden sidewalls always hide beneath the visible surface and that hundreds of distant bins do not create excessive render draw/CPU work. No fake resource count may be inferred from a visual level.
- **Fuel-bin subtype:** Dedicated Coal/Charcoal bins are **one** automatic fuel source, along with the available Coal/Charcoal held as finished products in **kilns and fires**. The fuel filter accepts **Coal and Charcoal only**; generic combustible materials and ordinary mixed storage are never eligible. **Coal and Charcoal cannot share one bin simultaneously.** An empty generic bin becomes a Coal Bin if its first item is Coal, or a Charcoal Bin if its first item is Charcoal; smelters and smithy forges may draw from either assigned type. After the bin is fully emptied it may be reassigned to the other resource. 'Coal/Charcoal Bin' is a description of these eligible assigned resource bins, not a special mixed-fuel exception or an additional blueprint.
- **Exact automation rules — smelters and smithy forges (latest creator decision):** Each consuming station may automatically pull fuel from a matching resource bin, kiln, or fire within **30 metres**, adjustable later. Source item types are **Coal and Charcoal only**. Run a collection pass **once every five minutes (300 seconds)**, NOT every frame. **No permissions checks** for these automatic transfers. Process consumers in **station creation order, oldest first**, with the first-created station taking its share before the next-created station; do not use round-robin or a fair-share lottery. Keep original creation ordering stable across saves/reloads. **If the consumer's hopper is already full, SKIP its check entirely**. Otherwise pull whatever eligible Coal/Charcoal is available up to free hopper capacity; later-created stations get only what remains. Sources outside 30 metres, ordinary chests/crates, ground items, and unrelated workstations do not qualify. No station ever auto-pulls wood.
- **Resource-bin sourcing other than furnace fuel:** Any future automatic station ingredient pull must be from an **appropriate dedicated bin**, not arbitrary chests or piles; do not automatically use kiln/fire contents as nonfuel recipe ingredients. This rule does not authorize automatic switching of Advanced Smelter recipes or moving extra carbon from the fuel hopper into the steel-input inventory.
- **Absolute item restriction:** Automatic fuel collection is restricted to item types **Coal and Charcoal**. Never auto-pull wood of any kind (sticks, kindling, branches, logs, blocks, firewood, planks or boards), or any other combustible material. Do not treat a generic `fuel` ingredient group as permission to pull wood.
- **Charcoal byproducts:** Applicable wood-burning fires/kilns may accumulate collectable Charcoal under approved rules. On a scheduled fuel check, a nearby smelter or smithy forge **may pull that already-produced, available Charcoal directly from the kiln or fire**, without an intermediate bin. Do **not** pull wood, transform unburned wood into Charcoal, or extract anything actively burning or already consumed; only actually available free stock may transfer. Manual retrieval/deposit remains available.

Automatic hopper replenishment must not silently feed carbon ingredients into the Advanced Smelter input or change a chosen iron recipe into steel.
- **Processing safety:** Pause cleanly for insufficient fuel/ore, **full output rack on Tiers 2 and 3 only**, missing human-powered bellows operation on Tier 2 when making iron, or insufficient wind on windmill-driven machinery; resume when the blocker clears. Tier 1 has no output inventory and instead drops ingots safely on the ground in front. Transfers from the three allowed fuel-source types must conserve quantities, be server-atomic under multiplayer and survive saves/restarts. **30 m range, 5-minute checks, no permission checks, oldest-station-first priority and full-hopper skip are settled design defaults** rather than numbers left for Claude to invent.
- **Smelter timing acceptance:** Primitive produces one ordinary ingot per **20 seconds**. Improved produces one ordinary ingot per **20 seconds** without a player working bellows, or ~**15.38 seconds** at **1.30× throughput** with bellows in use. Advanced, with sufficient wind at its nominal running rate, completes **2 ingots simultaneously every ~11.83 seconds** (aggregate ~5.92 seconds/ingot), equivalent to 1.69× per lane and 3.38× aggregate primitive throughput. Higher wind may increase speed per the later approved curve, while sub-threshold wind causes total pause and automatic restart. All tiers preserve 1 ore → 1 ingot or approved alloy material conservation; verify bronze pairs and queued batches cannot accidentally double output merely because two output lanes exist.
- **Smelter capacity and operator acceptance:** Tier 1 accepts at most 20 fuel and 20 ore/material units and places finished ingots on the ground in front, with **no output inventory**. Tier 2 accepts at most 40 fuel and 40 input units and holds at most **80 finished ingots on its output rack**. Without operating bellows, its copper/tin/bronze speed equals Tier 1 and iron is unavailable; while a player actively works bellows, base-metal throughput is **1.30×** and iron recipes can run; stepping away causes base metals to return to **1.00×** and active iron jobs to pause safely. Tier 3 accepts at most 80 fuel and 80 input units and holds at most **160 ingots**. It uses its integrated windmill/dual bellows automatically and stops entirely below the wind threshold. Test full racks stopping processing for Tiers 2/3, output retrieval and resume, dropped-ingot persistence for Tier 1, output conservation and multiplayer concurrency. Fuel-in-hopper is not steel's carbon ingredient.

## 5. Explicit item/progression quality rubric (run across every work package)

For each relevant item, recipe, station, material and quality tier answer, with evidence:

1. **Purpose:** What does this unlock, improve or enable for a player? Is the benefit discernible in gameplay?
2. **Timing:** Can this be acquired at the moment the guide introduces it, without unexplained resource/biome/season/tool gating?
3. **Dependency sanity:** Are inputs available and credible? Do quantities and station/tool requirements make sense and avoid accidental circularity or premium-material demands on basic objects?
4. **Tier value:** Does the next tier make a meaningful improvement rather than cost more for no benefit? If deliberately decorative, present it as an alternate, not a required power step.
5. **Station purpose:** Does each specialized or advanced station unlock unique production, genuine speed/scale/convenience, or a clear quality advantage? If not, mark it as potentially redundant, with options.
6. **Alternative routes:** Are currently approved alternatives retained (Iron Saw vs Sawmill, manual chopping vs Chopping Block), while the superseded old Stone Forge / no-bronze iron route is not silently reintroduced? Does the Primitive Smithy genuinely support copper/bronze tools and the Improved Smithy upgrade without circular prerequisites, and can the Improved Smithy make both the Iron Saw and Iron Anvil before they are needed for the Advanced Smithy, and can the Advanced Smithy forge the Steel Anvil before it is needed for the Master Smithy?
7. **Construction coherence:** Can a player complete a whole useful tier of building, including doors in doorway frames and proper roof pieces, before the guide moves on?
8. **Resource realism and supply:** Is an ordinary player able to source the amount without unreasonable busywork? Differentiate scarcity by deliberate design from accidental missing drops.
9. **System integration:** Does it use existing crafting/repair/placement/transport/storage/fuel/quality rules consistently, including server/save authority?
10. **Player instruction:** Can an inexperienced player reproduce the entire step with the actual controls and enough detail, but without massive repeated text?

Tag discrepancies as **DEFECT AGAINST EXISTING APPROVED RULE**, **DESIGN COHERENCE PROPOSAL**, **MISSING FEATURE**, **FUTURE/OUT OF SCOPE**, or **TEST NEEDED**. Apply existing decision authority by the actual change: defects against approved rules, implementation of already-approved missing features, and relevant verification can proceed within the authorized mission. A label such as MISSING FEATURE or TEST NEEDED is not itself an approval blocker. Item/recipe/station data changes stay under the item catalog freeze (`docs/ai-team/DECISION_AUTHORITY.md`) except where an approved design document specifies the change; values the documents leave open are not filled in silently (GOALS-REPAIR records any provisional value in `docs/ai-team/missions/GOALS-REPAIR/REPAIR_DECISIONS.md`). Escalate genuinely new or conflicting gameplay design, protected architecture/save changes, and unapproved recipe/economy choices; preserve their provenance and continue independent authorized work.
