# Dynasty — Missing Mechanics, Item Coherence & Full-Game Progression Repair Plan

**Status: REVIEW PLAN — creator-approved direction to FIX missing gameplay rather than delete tutorial goals. This file is NOT a blanket authorization to change approved item/recipe/progression rules or to implement/reprice the goals.**
**Date:** 13 November 2026. **Baseline reviewed:** `dev` merge `950af5ae3234b31ce430d91a603867fbc6413e4` (GOALS-AUDIT PR #1081). Revalidate against the actual HEAD at execution.

## 1. Objective, scope, and boundaries

Restore a coherent, genuinely playable path from first gathering to highest-tier production, using the audit as a defect inventory **and** an opportunity to assess whether every item family, tier, tool, workstation and construction step makes sense to a player.

- **FIX rather than remove:** Do not silently delete tutorial goals, simplify recipes to hide defects, or mark approved functional content FUTURE to make audit counts look better. Repair approved-but-broken acquisition/production/interaction mechanisms and make them testable.
- **Separate defect vs design choice:** Restore already-approved mechanics in code under their existing authoritative contracts; changes to item identity, tier order, role, recipe costs, unlocking policy or material substitution require an explicit creator decision. Keep a reasoned proposal, not an unapproved edit.
- **Guide, not gate:** Gameplay stays free-form. Goal order is instructional only, with optional branches, and no artificial recipe locks. This project is the complete game guide, NOT a short onboarding tutorial.
- **Rewards deferred:** Keep all coin reward amounts and the proposed 651-goal catalog unchanged during mechanics repairs. The audit's 3,059 vs 15,670 copper models are preliminary and time-untested. A later separately authorized economy pass will tune payouts.
- **Existing design decisions remain in force:** Planks and Hardwood Boards never substitute; Iron/Steel hand saws and Sawmill have equal lumber yields (different speed); the creator's newer copper → bronze → improved smithy/bellows → iron route replaces the earlier optional-bronze/no-bronze-required first Iron Saw route; reconcile the old master catalog before implementation; doors are separate from empty doorway frames; approved roof slopes and door configuration rules stand; no unauthorized item renaming or adding stations.
- **Versioned evidence:** `main` stays stable. Use the established feature-branch → review → merge-to-`dev` gate, with small PRs and clear rollback. Actual gameplay changes require tests and the creator's normal development approvals.

### Creator-directed smelting progression and automatic workstation sourcing (13 Nov 2026)

**Latest creator decisions govern; do not restore superseded automation or wind rules.** These are design instructions for a later implementation, not evidence of already-working gameplay. Automatic fuel sources are **appropriate bins, kilns and fires**, with the explicit timing/range/priority below. Exact recipes for stations and bin sizes, item visuals, wind-start threshold and some processing rates remain separate tuning/approval questions.

- **Creator-approved simplified smelting rules (13 Nov):** The game **does not simulate or track temperature**. A single ordinary ore input produces **one ingot of the same metal** (1 Copper Ore → 1 Copper Ingot; 1 Tin Ore → 1 Tin Ingot; 1 Iron Ore → 1 Iron Ingot at a smelter tier capable of iron). For compatible two-metal alloys, input 1 unit of each ore and obtain **two alloy ingots**; explicitly **1 Copper Ore + 1 Tin Ore → 2 Bronze Ingots**. Fuel to run the furnace is separate from these material conversions. Do not reuse the older 3 Copper Ingots + 1 Tin Ingot → 4 Bronze Ingots rule or the current multi-ore Iron Ingot rule as if they remain approved. Any other real alloy combination must be separately defined and approved, not inferred automatically from all metals.
- **Steel in the Tier 3 Blast Furnace:** The **fuel hopper** holds Coal/Charcoal to run the furnace. To **make steel**, the player also puts **Coal OR Charcoal directly in the ore/material INPUT inventory together with iron**. That second Coal/Charcoal quantity is a **consumed carbon ingredient**, distinct from fuel in the hopper. The Blast Furnace then automatically produces **Steel Ingots** using its normal queue/batching. If the input contains only iron, produce Iron Ingots, not steel; if carbon is present in the INPUT with iron and a valid steel batch is selected, produce Steel Ingots. The steel output count stays tied to iron input, not increased by wind or by adding carbon. For initial implementation planning, treat the iron feedstock as **Iron Ore** (matching the user's ore-to-ingot input pattern); whether **Iron Ingots** may also be converted into steel is an unresolved optional route, not an automatic assumption. Likewise the exact Coal/Charcoal quantity consumed as carbon per iron ore is still to be selected; do **not** silently invent it.
- **Operating model:** Smelter capability is governed by the station tier and airflow equipment, NOT a tracked heat/temperature variable. The Bellows tier uses manual operation for its improved processing; **a windmill-powered station operates ONLY while enough wind is blowing to keep its windmill moving**. When wind is insufficient it **stops production entirely**, retaining the unfinished batch and resources, and **resumes automatically** when wind increases enough. While moving, stronger wind increases throughput. Inputs, fuel and outputs occupy separate inventories and transfers must be item-conserving. All furnaces stop/pause on insufficient fuel, missing recipe ingredients, or full outputs.
- **Tier 1 — Primitive Smelting Furnace:** stone and clay construction with a tall chimney and natural draft; handles lower-temperature copper, tin and bronze production. Ore input, coal/charcoal fuel hopper and ingot output.
- **Tier 1 — Primitive Smithy (first metalworking shop):** buildable using only materials and stone tools obtainable at the start (stone, clay, wooden blocks and logs, per creator's stated concept). No iron, bronze, nails, boards or preexisting metal parts required. It uses a simple charcoal fire and stone anvil, and shapes copper ingots into the first copper tools and bronze ingots into early bronze tools and fittings. It **does not smelt ore**. Confirm exactly which copper tools exist, or propose new approved definitions, and check access to copper/tin with stone gathering tools.
- **Tier 2 — Bellows Smelter:** metal fittings (e.g. pins/hinges) allow manually worked bellows to increase furnace heat and process iron. Design how pumping heat is maintained without repetitive busywork.
- **Tier 2 — Improved Smithy:** built after bronze production, with an improved forge, bellows and **bronze anvil**; can work copper/bronze and forge both the **Iron Saw** and **Iron Anvil** from iron ingots produced by the Bellows Smelter. Ironworking on a bronze anvil causes accelerated wear, not an arbitrary prohibition. The Iron Saw unlocks Planks and Hardwood Boards. The Iron Anvil is needed to build the Advanced Smithy, not its own manufacture.
- **Smithy wear, breakage and repair (creator decisions, 13 Nov 2026):** Every smithy has persistent workstation durability. Ordinary working wears the station; working a harder/higher-tier metal using a lower-tier anvil (e.g. iron at the Improved Smithy's Bronze Anvil) increases its wear. At **zero durability**, the smithy becomes **broken but stays physically placed**; it cannot be opened or used via its normal workstation interaction. Interacting normally must show the exact warning **"Needs repair"** instead of opening the crafting/inventory UI. **When an ordinary tool hammer is equipped, right-click provides a hammer action menu with a selectable `Repair` function**. This is the repair interaction used on broken smithies, and it MUST remain available even when the smithy's regular interaction is blocked. **ALL ordinary tool-class hammers, including Wooden, Stone, Bronze, Iron and Steel Hammers, can perform this repair; WAR HAMMERS ARE EXCLUDED.** Do not turn a normal left-click or workstation-open interaction into the repair action; the player deliberately chooses `Repair` via the hammer's right-click controls. Do not introduce a special Repair Hammer item, invent a new hammer tier, or treat hammer-shaped melee weapons as construction/repair tools. Repair restores the smithy's usable condition while preserving its actual station identity and contents, add-ons, inventories and persistent state; no deletion, duplicated contents or need to rebuild the smithy. Exact repair material cost, amount restored, and hammer durability consumption remain design/balancing questions, not silently chosen constants. Ensure an early ordinary hammer is accessible before the first smithy can fail and that a player can invoke repair even though the smithy's normal UI is locked.
- **Smithy repair acceptance tests:** Verify ordinary metalworking wear and accelerated bronze-anvil wear when crafting iron; at zero durability the model stays in place, crafting/normal inventory interactions are unavailable, and players see **"Needs repair"**. Verify that each ordinary hammer tool can repair, but a **War Hammer cannot**. Test the equipped ordinary hammer's **right-click → Repair** option against a broken smithy, confirm that normal workstation interaction remains blocked, then verify persistence across save/reload, player reacquisition of usability, and conservation of station contents/resources. Do not replace normal equipment/tool-repair mechanics with this station rule.
- **Tier 3 smelter — Windmill-powered Blast Furnace is ONE complete buildable station:** Its windmill and **two mechanically driven bellows are integrated into the same station model, blueprint and build cost**; do NOT require a separately placed Windmill item, a windmill-to-furnace connection, a detachable bellows system, or an external power hookup. The unit processes queued batches automatically only while the integrated windmill turns. **Insufficient wind = no rotation and ZERO production**; resume automatically when wind returns, without losing queued batches or partial work. Higher available wind raises processing speed when running, never ore-to-ingot yield. **General rule:** any game station whose operation depends on an integrated windmill stops when wind is too weak to rotate it and restarts once sufficient; no imaginary backup drive or calm-weather minimum production. The precise wind threshold, speed curve and visual animation are to be tuned rather than invented as already approved.
- **Tier 3 — Advanced Smithy:** constructed using the Iron Anvil made at the Improved Smithy; contains a capable forge, bellows, **iron anvil**, and tool racks; handles normal ironwork and can work steel as steel ingots become available from the Blast Furnace. **It crafts the Steel Anvil**, the required major component for the final Master Smithy. Do not give the Advanced Smithy the quencher: that is now reserved for the Master Smithy under the creator's latest direction.
- **Tier 4 — Master Smithy:** the **more ornate, top-tier workshop** equipped with a **steel anvil** (forged at the Advanced Smithy), the **Quenching Trough/quencher**, and other high-quality tools. It supports the previously approved masterwork and craftsmanship roles, with detailed tempering/quality effects to be reconciled against previous catalog rules. It does not smelt ore or ingots. The Steel Anvil must NOT require the Master Smithy for its own manufacture.
- **Progression no-cycle rule:** stone tools → Primitive Smithy + Primitive Smelter → copper tools → bronze → Improved Smithy → bellows-driven iron → Iron Saw + Iron Anvil → Advanced Smithy → wind-driven Blast Furnace/steel → **Steel Anvil crafted at Advanced Smithy** → **Master Smithy**. Ensure all Bronze Smithy-era requirements (bronze fittings, any leather bellows), Blast Furnace components, Iron Anvil, Steel Anvil, and building recipes are accessible without relying on their downstream workstations. Names and tier arrangement here reflect the creator's latest choice, superseding older contradictory rules; exact quantities and visuals remain for review.
- **New buildable dedicated resource-bin family (creator direction, 13 Nov 2026):** Bins are constructed from **Planks and Nails**, with larger sizes using more materials (exact recipes still to be designed). They are dedicated to a compatible stored resource rather than general mixed-inventory chests; the visible contents resemble the assigned material, and the material pile rises as the stored count increases. **Fixed capacities, measured in stored resource units:** **Small Bin = 1,000**, **Medium Bin = 5,000**, **Large Bin = 10,000**. These capacities apply to resource bins including fuel bins; do not reinterpret as weight, inventory slots or stack counts. Expose actual quantities; enforce capacity, real deposits/withdrawals and persistence. **No permission check applies to the new automatic workstation pulls**; do not conflate this specific resource automation rule with the separately approved player-facing locks/access rules for ordinary crates and chests. Exact resource assignment/locking policy, model families, build recipes, footprint, and fill-display thresholds remain to be reviewed.
- **Visual resource-bin design — creator-approved, 13 Nov 2026:** Each size is a **solid-sided, enclosed wooden container with an open top only**, built of Planks and Nails. Produce **one empty bin mesh for each of Small, Medium and Large**; the wood sides and floor must be opaque/solid enough that no player can see inside from the side or beneath the bin. **Render only the TOP SURFACE of the stored resource as a separate mesh**, positioned within the open-top interior. Godot moves that surface up/down on the bin's local Y axis according to `stored_count / capacity`: **0 = hide surface; intermediate quantities = surface lower/higher inside the walls; full = surface near the rim** (optionally a realistic mound that stays visually contained). There is **no need for an additional 3D fill volume, mesh underneath the surface, or separate empty/25%/50%/75%/full mesh variants** because solid wooden walls conceal the empty space. Resource surface geometry and textures should correspond to the assigned stored material (e.g. coal chunks, charcoal, sand, grain, ore, stone); visually similar resources can reuse geometry with different materials. This is an **art pipeline plan**, not a request to generate concept art now: create reference images for Meshy only when the creator explicitly requests them. Animate no per-item objects; update surface position only on actual inventory changes, not each frame; batch, cull or LOD distant visuals as appropriate. Long goods needing racks/pallets are separate approved storage families, not reasons to turn these enclosed bins into open-sided racks.
- **Visual bin acceptance:** At `0 / capacity`, material mesh is absent. At `capacity / 2`, only the half-height top surface is visible through the opening; at full, the surface is near the rim without visibly floating from any exterior view. Confirm consistent height mapping across all three capacities (1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000), correct resource surface material on selection, and persistence after reload. Validate that wooden sidewalls always hide beneath the visible surface and that hundreds of distant bins do not create excessive render draw/CPU work. No fake resource count may be inferred from a visual level.
- **Fuel-bin subtype:** Dedicated Coal/Charcoal bins are **one** automatic fuel source, along with the available Coal/Charcoal held as finished products in **kilns and fires**. The fuel filter accepts **Coal and Charcoal only**; generic combustible materials and ordinary mixed storage are never eligible. Whether Coal and Charcoal use separate dedicated bins or one compatible combined bin remains a naming/assignment decision.
- **Exact automation rules — smelters and smithy forges (latest creator decision):** Each consuming station may automatically pull fuel from a matching resource bin, kiln, or fire within **30 metres**, adjustable later. Source item types are **Coal and Charcoal only**. Run a collection pass **once every five minutes (300 seconds)**, NOT every frame. **No permissions checks** for these automatic transfers. Process consumers in **station creation order, oldest first**, with the first-created station taking its share before the next-created station; do not use round-robin or a fair-share lottery. Keep original creation ordering stable across saves/reloads. **If the consumer's hopper is already full, SKIP its check entirely**. Otherwise pull whatever eligible Coal/Charcoal is available up to free hopper capacity; later-created stations get only what remains. Sources outside 30 metres, ordinary chests/crates, ground items, and unrelated workstations do not qualify. No station ever auto-pulls wood.
- **Resource-bin sourcing other than furnace fuel:** Any future automatic station ingredient pull must be from an **appropriate dedicated bin**, not arbitrary chests or piles; do not automatically use kiln/fire contents as nonfuel recipe ingredients. - **Absolute item restriction:** Automatic fuel collection is restricted to item types **Coal and Charcoal**. Never auto-pull wood of any kind (sticks, kindling, branches, logs, blocks, firewood, planks or boards), or any other combustible material. Do not treat a generic `fuel` ingredient group as permission to pull wood.

Manual retrieval/deposit remains available.

Automatic hopper replenishment must not silently feed carbon ingredients into the Blast Furnace input or change a chosen iron recipe into steel.

## 5. Explicit item/progression quality rubric (run across every work package)

For each relevant item, recipe, station, material and quality tier answer, with evidence:

1. **Purpose:** What does this unlock, improve or enable for a player? Is the benefit discernible in gameplay?
2. **Timing:** Can this be acquired at the moment the guide introduces it, without unexplained resource/biome/season/tool gating?
3. **Dependency sanity:** Are inputs available and credible? Do quantities and station/tool requirements make sense and avoid accidental circularity or premium-material demands on basic objects?
4. **Tier value:** Does the next tier make a meaningful improvement rather than cost more for no benefit? If deliberately decorative, present it as an alternate, not a required power step.
5. **Station purpose:** Does each specialized or advanced station unlock unique production, genuine speed/scale/convenience, or a clear quality advantage? If not, mark it as potentially redundant, with options.
6. **Alternative routes:** Are currently approved alternatives retained (Iron Saw vs Sawmill, manual chopping vs Chopping Block), while the superseded old Stone Forge / no-bronze iron route is not silently reintroduced? Does the Primitive Smithy genuinely support copper/bronze tools and the Improved Smithy upgrade without circular prerequisites, and can the Improved Smithy make both the Iron Saw and Iron Anvil before they are needed for the Advanced Smithy, and can the Advanced Smithy forge the Steel Anvil before it is needed for the Master Smithy?
7. **Construction coherence:** Can a player complete a whole useful tier of building, including doors in doorway frames and proper roof pieces, before the guide moves on?
8. **Resource realism and supply:** Is an ordinary player able to source the amount without unreasonable busywork? Differentiate scarcity by deliberate design from accidental missing drops.
9. **System integration:** Does it use existing crafting/repair/placement/transport/storage/fuel/quality rules consistently, including server/save authority?
10. **Player instruction:** Can an inexperienced player reproduce the entire step with the actual controls and enough detail, but without massive repeated text?

Tag discrepancies as **DEFECT AGAINST EXISTING APPROVED RULE**, **DESIGN COHERENCE PROPOSAL**, **MISSING FEATURE**, **FUTURE/OUT OF SCOPE**, or **TEST NEEDED**. Only the first category can be fixed without further design approval when evidence is unambiguous. Every other change must retain original approved wording and request a targeted creator decision.
