# World resource distribution: implementation

**Status:** stages 1 and 2 and the code half of stage 3 implemented on branch `claude/project-thread-1mwrkm` (not yet tested on a lane, not yet played). Lane measurements and pictures are still to come.
**Design source:** Dave, 21 Nov 2026, `docs/handoff/RESOURCE_DISTRIBUTION_ARCHITECTURE.md` (dev 53fb11a9) and his assignment in project thread "Implement world resource distribution". That handoff is the requirement; this file maps it onto the code and records the provisional values (RD-n). Values here are PROVISIONAL unless marked otherwise.

## How it fits the existing game

| Handoff responsibility | Where it lives now |
|---|---|
| Landscape context (island, interior, rivers, hills, water) | `src/stream/resource_context.gd`, built once a run from the baked tiles' coarse maps (`low_height`, `low_water_class`, 32 m) on a 64 m grid, on a worker thread at server start (`server_items.start_resources`). Islands, distance to the sea, each island's widest distance and peak, the high ground near each cell, a priority-flood drainage (receivers and drained area) that follows tributaries and confluences. |
| Resource availability | Same file: `journey` (shore 0 to headwaters 1) and one `field` per metal (iron, copper, tin). |
| Deposit generation | Same file: finite sources per metal on a world grid, with size, radius and a stable id; the starting-island check adds an assured source where needed. |
| Surface representation | `server_items.gd`: `_lay_out_sources` (outcrops at a source, fragments downhill), `_lay_out_ore_rocks` (copper, tin and iron rocks now limited to their area or their river clue, sizes and shapes by journey), `_lay_out_boulders` (plain rocks bigger, commoner and more angular inland). Shapes on the client: `ore_rock_look.gd` (rounded, broken, jagged) chosen by the row's `ang`. |
| Associated discoveries | Same file: coal districts and seams, and pockets per source from an association table; `server_items.gd`: seam outcrops (`_lay_out_seams`), coal from a dig in a seam (`_dig_seam_coal`), pockets opened by a dig (`_open_pockets`, saved in `world_items.json` "pockets") and laid as a rock in the hole (`_place_pocket`). |
| Validation and persistence | Deterministic from the seed and the tiles: nothing new is saved for the landscape. Worked and taken rocks keep the existing saves (`boulders`, `_taken` keys). Starting-island route check in `_assure`. |

The existing geology (tile ore bodies, coal layers, gem lenses) stays as it is: it is in the baked tiles, and changing it would mean a new `TERRAIN_VERSION` and a re-bake. Its copper, tin and iron outcrops now show only inside that metal's area (`_boulder_ore`). **No terrain version change and no bake.**

## Stage 1 (this PR): landscape, areas, sources, river clues, rock form

1. **Journey** (RD-1): `0.5 x inland + 0.25 x height share + 0.25 x high ground share`, each 0..1 against the island's own widest inland distance (at least 1,500 m) and its peak (at least 150 m); high ground = the highest ground within 768 m. Not absolute height alone, not world-origin distance.
2. **Areas, the paint** (RD-2): per metal, `journey + 0.55 x (warped value noise - 0.5)` (waves 3.2 km, warped by 1.6 km), cut at the island's own quantile so iron covers 90%, copper 60% and tin 40% of a large island, blended over a narrow band. Different noise per metal, so the areas overlap irregularly and are not rings. Small islands get less (none below about 400 m inland width; iron never under 60% of its share). Dave's 80%/60% were examples; these shares are tuning values.
   On the starting island (96 km², peak 1,375 m): iron 90% (100% since RD-16), copper 60%, tin 40%; inside the tin area iron is present in 99.8% and copper in 96%; inside copper, iron in 96%.
3. **Sources** (RD-3): one candidate per metal per 384 m square, a source with chance `base x field x (0.4 + 0.6 x journey)`, base iron 0.10, copper 0.22, tin 0.22. Size 0..1 grows with journey; radius 8 to 30 m. Never in the sea, a lake or a river cell. Starting island today (cloud mirror of the code, not a lane run): about 130 iron, 200 copper, 150 tin sources over the nine baked tiles.
4. **Starting-island check** (RD-4, handoff 13): walking cost from spawn over the island (`64 m x (1 + 8 x slope²)`, +150 m per river cell, slopes over 0.7 refused, ground above 60% of the island peak refused). If no source of a metal lies within its budget (iron 3 km, copper 3 km, tin 6 km), the best reachable cell of its area becomes an assured source. Today: nearest iron 0.6 km, copper 1.4 km, tin 2.0 km by route from spawn, highest ground on the tin route about 140 m (peak 1,375 m), so nothing is assured. Logged at server start ("resources: ...").
5. **River clues** (RD-5): from each source down its own drainage; strength 1 at the source, falling by `exp(-step / transport)` along a channel (iron 3 km, copper 1.8 km, tin 1.2 km) and `exp(-step / 300 m)` down a hillside; a lake ends it; nothing runs up another tributary. Copper and tin rocks by water count the clue like their area; their size shrinks with the clue (down to 0.4) so finds get smaller downstream.
6. **Copper, tin and iron rocks** (RD-6): the shared `ore_rocks` rule stays (Dave 21 Nov: rivers, more and bigger toward the mountains, a little on land, very many over their own ore body), with three changes: `upland` is the journey, not height; every chance is scaled by the metal's area (by water: by area or river clue, whichever is higher); a mountain-foot boulder needs the area. Outside its area and away from its clues an ore does not appear. **This supersedes the 21 Nov "both should be there [near the ocean] ... small chance almost anywhere" for copper and tin where they conflict**: the handoff is Dave's later direction ("copper and tin should retain their inland identity"; tin by the sea only as a source-linked river find). Iron stays almost everywhere. Meteorites unchanged.
7. **Sources on the ground** (RD-7): at a source, 2 to 8 angular outcrop boulders inside its radius (0.5 to 1.4 m radius by size), grade 0.4 to 0.8 by size; below it 4 to 14 broken fragments strewn down the slope up to 40 to 120 m, smaller the farther, the smaller ones loose ore to pick up by hand. Same rules for iron (its outcrops still need a bronze or better pick). Keys: cell slots 64 and up.
8. **Rock form** (RD-8): every boulder row gets `ang` 0..1 (journey plus a little jitter, water-worn rocks x 0.45). The client draws ore rocks rounded under 0.35, broken (the photos' shape, unchanged) up to 0.7, jagged above; plain boulders keep their rounded mesh unless jagged. Plain boulders: chance x 0.6 at the shore to x 1.5 at the headwaters, radius x 0.55 to x 1.25 (so the biggest common boulder is about 3 m across at the headwaters, provisional against Dave's open choice "3 m common, 5 m landmarks").

## The layered cake (Dave 27 Nov 2026, R-RES-LAYERS, BugsList 186)

Dave restated the paint as a tiered cake seen from above: iron as small rocks everywhere; then bigger iron and small copper; then bigger iron and copper and small tin; the top layer everything big (boulders, veins, surface and underground), with that rich layer "right around the bottom of the mountain or on the first plateau", and still good resources farther up. What changed (RD-16, provisional):
- **Rich** (`distribution.rich_*`): per 64 m cell, `smooth(0, 0.55, journey) x (1 - 0.25 x smooth(0.25, 0.5, height / island peak))`: 0 at the shore, 1 at the mountain's foot and on the first plateau, 0.75 high on the mountain. Ore rocks (by-water chance from 30% to 80% a try, sizes) and sources (chance x0.4 to x1, size) read rich in place of the journey, and so do ore rocks' shapes. The areas, plain boulders, rock fields and pockets keep the journey.
- **Copper and tin on dry land**: 2.5% a try inside their area (was 1%), so small rocks lie across the whole layer, not only by water. Still none outside the area (no copper on the beach).
- **Iron**: area 100% of the island (was 90%), so small iron lies by the shore too; iron stones are one-ore by the shore and come in two- and three-ore sizes going up (50/30/20 at full rich).
- **Danger inland** (his words): bears, wolves and foxes may den in caves (`animals.json` cave_den_claim) and goblins live in river-side caves; nothing scales danger with distance inland yet (goblins are the NPC stage).
- **Gold, silver, gems** (R-RES-PRECIOUS; Dave's go 28 Nov 2026): the dig pockets below (item 10) and, since RD-17, gold and silver veins with gems deeper down ("Gold and silver veins" below).

## Stage 2: coal seams and finds revealed by digging

9. **Coal districts and seams** (RD-9, boulders.json `distribution.coal`): a district is dry land on a hillside (rise 0.08 to 0.25 per metre on the 64 m grid) within 400 m of a lake, river, marsh, swamp, fen or bog, where its own noise (1.6 km waves) passes a cut, so not every such slope has coal. Rivers count because streams cut down through coal beds and show them in their banks. In a district, one seam candidate per 256 m square becomes a seam with chance 0.6 x district. A seam is a coal layer 1.2 to 2.5 m thick at the ground height of its centre, 60 to 200 m along the contour, reaching 80 m into the hill. Where the ground meets the layer (searched 13 m up and down the slope every 6 m, so every read stays inside the cell's loaded ground) the server lays a near-black coal outcrop (ore coal, grade 0.5, radius 0.4 to 0.8 m) with 1 to 3 loose coal a few metres below, so a seam reads as a dark band along the slope with crumbs below. 22 Nov 2026: this was every 14 m, radius 0.5 to 1.0 m, 0 to 2 loose coal, dark grey; the gate pictures showed only a few dark boulders. Outcrop coal per seam rose about 17%. A shovel dig inside the layer gives 1 to 2 coal (plus the shovel tier's extra, times the shovel size) on top of the ground's own yield, so a player can follow it into the hill; the first time says "A black seam of coal runs through the hillside here. Follow it into the hill." A seam is a bounded body (handoff 14): it holds 2 coal per metre of its length (120 to 400), what is left is saved with the world (`world_items.json` "seams"), and it does not grow back. The geology's own coal layers and their outcrops are unchanged. Starting island today (cloud mirror, not a lane run): 51 seams, about 9% of the island in coal districts, nearest seam about 1.2 km by route from spawn.
10. **Pockets** (RD-10, `distribution.pockets` and `pocket_rock`): per source, each row of the association table it fits rolls once with chance x (0.5 + the source's size). Rows (chance): placer gold in the river gravels 1 to 8 cells down any source (0.06), gold in quartz veins at inland copper and iron sources, journey 0.6+ (0.04), silver at copper sources, journey 0.3+ (0.12), amethyst at sources journey 0.45+ (0.05), agate at iron and copper sources up to journey 0.7 (0.06), garnet at sources journey 0.75+ in the hard mountain rock (0.08), clear quartz (Crystal lens) at sources journey 0.3+ (0.06). A pocket lies 0.4 to 2.5 m under the generated ground within 1.5 m across. A dig that reaches it opens it: "You dig into a pocket of Gold nugget. Break it out with a pick or the Stone Hammer." Its rock (0.3 to 0.45 m, 6 to 12 stones, grade by find) lies in the hole, is saved with the world (`world_items.json` "pockets": id -> where the rock lies; what is left keeps under its key in "boulders") and breaks into its find with any pick or the Stone Hammer. Most sources have none. Starting island today (cloud mirror): 22 pockets (gold 6, agate 5, quartz 4, silver 3, amethyst 3, garnet 1); 102 on the nine tiles.
11. **The five new finds** (coordinator decision D-ORE-1, 21 Nov 2026, under Dave's rule that agents' questions go to the coordinator): items 1518 Gold nugget (value 200 copper, 0.05 kg), 1519 Silver ore (40, 1.5 kg), 1520 Amethyst (60, 0.03 kg), 1521 Garnet (50, 0.02 kg), 1522 Agate (25, 0.05 kg). Sell value only (Hob buys materials), no recipes. Values PROVISIONAL: a gold nugget at two silver coins against an iron ingot's 30 copper, silver ore above copper and tin ore, gems between the Crystal lens (15) and gold. Icons are recoloured from the copper ore, iron ore and crystal lens icons (tools/recolour_icons.py); a bake can replace them. Their rock in the world uses the outcrop shader with its own tint and streaks (inventory_view.gd OUTCROP_LOOK).
## Stage 3: diagnostics, access, tuning

12. **Known dangers on the starting-island routes** (RD-12, handoff 13 "use existing danger information"): at start the server passes the camps of goblin tribes that will not trade (attitude trades false; none today, Ragtooth is wary and trades) and every bandit site whose camp stands (stage 3), each with `danger_m` 200 m round it; the route check refuses those cells, so an assured source is never placed only behind one. Read once at start: a bandit camp that wakes later does not move a source. No new danger is made. Wolves and bears are not counted (they range; their dens are made near players as they go).
13. **Diagnostics** (handoff 15): `src/worldgen/tools/resource_map.gd` builds the landscape from a world's tiles exactly as the server does and writes `resource_areas.png` (areas, overlaps, sources, assured sources, the ground within the tin route budget of spawn, dangers), `resource_finds.png` (river clues, coal districts, seams, pockets by find) and `resource_report.txt`. Headless, no rendering: `godot --headless --path . --script res://src/worldgen/tools/resource_map.gd -- --out=DIR [--around=6000] [--seed=N] [--spawn=X,Z] [--danger=X,Z,R]`. Adverse seeds: run it with other `--seed` values.

## Stage 4: the handoff 13 checks

15. **Enough copper and tin for the first bronze** (RD-13, handoff 13 "accessible copper and tin quantities cover the intended first bronze equipment and required construction costs"; `distribution.first_bronze`, `ore_margin`). The kit: the seven bronze tools (pickaxe, axe, hammer, knife, saw, shovel, chisel) and the two stations the way to iron requires (Improved smelter, Improved smithy). `resource_context.first_bronze_need` reads every amount from `recipes.json`, `blueprints.json` and `structures.json` smelting: today 13 + 6 Bronze ingots, plus 40 nails and 4 hinges in one forge batch each = 21 ingots, so 11 Copper ore and 11 Tin ore a player (1 + 1 ore make 2 ingots). Times 32 players (a full server) and a margin of 2 = 704 of each. The starting-island check counts the ore pieces in the outcrops of every source within the route budget (`source_ore`: the server's own outcrop count, size and grade; ore is mined by weight, so a 1.5 kg piece takes 3 stones of the rock; fragments and loose pieces not counted) and adds an assured source, up to three, while short. Logged at start: "resources: tin within 6000 m of spawn: N sources (1 wanted), about X ore ...: enough". Finding: on the shipped world quantity is not the limit. One smallest source holds only about 150 ore pieces in its outcrops (so an island with one tiny source gets more), a mid-size one about 3,800; across seeds 1 to 200 the least copper within budget was about 2,900 pieces and the least tin about 31,000 (cloud mirror). Weapons and armour are not counted.
16. **Several iron locations before iron smelting** (RD-14, handoff 13; `distribution.known_sources`, iron 3): at least three iron sources within the iron budget (3 km by route), else assured ones are added. Across seeds 1 to 200, 38 seeds had fewer than three and 2 had none (now fixed by this rule). Besides sources, iron shows from the first hour as rust-red river stones, bog iron and rocks over 90% of the island.
17. **Charcoal and coal fit the bellows smelting** (handoff 13 and the charcoal rule): checked against the live data in `test_resource_landscape` 10b. The coal the seams and coal rocks give is smelter fuel and both smelters take it beside Charcoal. The iron jobs want bellows and the Improved smelter has them. A Primitive kiln (100 Charcoal a log) and the Primitive smelter need no metal, so charcoal never waits on coal. The charcoal route runs through the real server in `test_bronze_path` and `test_iron_saw_route`, and coal in a smelter's hopper in `test_smelters`. Not run end to end: seam coal through a lit Improved smelter into an iron ingot in one test (the pieces are tested apart). Goals (#1468) lead charcoal, then copper and tin, then the Improved smelter and bog iron, which fits.
18. **Inland reward, measured** (handoff 13; seeds 1 to 200, cloud mirror). By journey third (shore, middle, headwaters):

    - Mean ore pieces in a source's outcrops: about 2,700 near the shore, 7,000 in the middle and 17,000 at the headwaters, for every metal.
    - Plain rocks are larger and commoner inland (RD-8).
19. **Host rock for coal** (RD-15, handoff "use sedimentary host rock where the world supports it"; `distribution.coal.host_families`): a seam whose centre lies in igneous or metamorphic layered rock of the baked tile (granite, basalt, tuff, slate, marble) has no outcrops and no coal. The server reads `TileTerrainData.bedrock_name_at` at the seam's centre and height once its tile is in memory; unknown rock never stops one. How many seams this keeps near spawn is NOT MEASURED (it needs the tiles' province geology, a lane run).

## Server start and the network

- Every scatter cell (stones and sticks too) waits until the landscape is built on its worker thread, so no cell is ever laid without it. The build time is logged ("resources: N tiles read and built in X ms"); measured on Lane B (Linux, the slow lane machine) at 7.9 s for the nine tiles, 22 Nov 2026, on a worker thread: the main thread keeps running, but no stones, sticks or rocks are laid out around a player who joins in those first seconds until it is done. A cell near a coal seam also waits for the tile under the seam's centre, whose ground sets the seam's height.
- Boulder rows sent to clients carry a few more keys (`ang`; `source`, `seam` or `pocket` on those rocks) and plain boulders are up to 1.5 times commoner at the headwaters: the world-item traffic grows a little, NOT MEASURED. No protocol change: an older client ignores the keys.
- The area noise is seeded from each metal's name with Godot's String.hash(): an engine upgrade that changed that hash would move the areas (as it would every other seeded layout that uses it).

## Saves and existing worlds

Nothing is regenerated and no world is reset. Two save fields are added to `world_items.json` ("pockets", "seams"); an older file without them loads as before. Scatter is not saved; it is laid out again from the seed each run, as before. In an existing world, rocks nobody has touched move to the new layout. A rock that was partly worked keeps its saved count under its key, capped at what the rock now at that key holds (the same as every scatter change since #1411). Loose pieces already taken stay taken by key.

## Gold and silver veins (RD-17, PROVISIONAL, 28 Nov 2026)
Dave 27 Nov 2026, go 28 Nov (R-RES-PRECIOUS): veins "along side the deeper iron, tin veins under the mountains or up in the mountains", spread "to entice the players to travel, make roads, build caravans and enterprise around building mines", gems "discovered by a percentage chance as you mine deeper". `resource_context._veins`, settings `boulders.json` distribution `veins`:
- Where: an iron or tin source at journey 0.5 or farther in (the rich layer at the mountain foot, the first plateau and the mountains) starts a vein with chance 0.35 x (0.5 + its size); no vein starts within 1,500 m of another, so they lie far apart. Silver 55%, gold 45%.
- Shape: from the source's edge it runs uphill, under the mountain, 120 to 300 m: a pocket every 10 m. The first 3 lie on the ground as outcrop rocks (0.5 to 0.8 m, 20 to 40 stones, grade x 0.3), laid with the cell with no dig and saved like a dug pocket. The rest are buried, 0.6 m down at the shallow end to 3.5 m at the deep end; a dig within 2 m that reaches the depth opens one as any pocket (item 10).
- Gems: 0.8 m under each buried pocket, a gem with chance 0.06 x the pocket's depth (1 m down 6%, 3 m down 18%): agate, amethyst or clear quartz (Crystal lens), garnet too at journey 0.75 and over.
- Mining: as every pocket rock, any pick or the Stone Hammer. Finds are sell value only (D-ORE-1): no recipes, coins or trade yet.
- Map check: the look lines name the nearest `gold vein outcrop`, `silver vein`, gem and pocket of each find by route (Lane A).
No terrain version change, no bake, no world reset: veins are worked out at server start like the pockets.
