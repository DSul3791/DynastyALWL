# What Dave sees: MikeW's animal PRs in plain words

One short paragraph per PR: what you will see in the game with its switch on. Every switch is off until its PC check passes; with
it off, the game looks as it does today. `--life=name,name` turns switches on for a session, `--life-layer-off` turns them all off.

**PR 1, eye shine (#596).** At night, with a torch in your hand, the eyes of deer, wolves, foxes, cats and horses glow back at you in
their own colours: green-white, yellow-green, green, blue-green.

**PR 2, wet look.** Animals that have swum or stood in rain have darker, glossier coats that dry off slowly.

**PR 3, always-on looks.** The animals come alive standing still: tails wag and swish at flies, ears turn toward you, chests rise and
fall, heads lift and follow you, deer graze facing roughly one way and look up together, animals lie down and sleep at night,
rabbits and kangaroos hop, a black bear pops its jaw before it charges, and the dog sits and lies by you. Animals with painted-on
eyes get glossy eyes.

**PR 4, footfall and feet.** Running hooves throw up little puffs of dust, mud or snow; animals splash through water; they leave
prints in snow and mud; their feet sit on uneven ground and their bodies tip with the slope.

**PR 5, predators and flight.** A stalking wolf creeps low with its tail still and freezes when a deer looks up; a fox or lynx leaps
on its prey; ears lie back and tails blow in a gale; geese, ducks and pheasants take off, flap and glide.

**PR 6, coats, young, water and idles.** Deer turn greyer in winter and foxes paler; fur ripples in the wind; a horse that rolls gets
dusty; fawns, foals and calves are small, take quick little steps and wobble when newborn; animals drink at the water's edge, swim
low with their legs paddling and shake off on the bank; standing animals scratch, shake, stretch, groom and look round.

**PR 7, herd groups (#666, server).** A herd moves as one: a leader drifts as it grazes, the others keep their places, and they
scatter on alarm and come back together.

**PR 8, flight part 2 and wiring.** Birds lean into their turns and a raven rolls; a goblin backing away from your dog walks
backwards properly; an animal warning your dog looks at the dog, not at you.

**PR 9, young part 2, new models, voices, night eyes.** Young animals grow over the year, stay by their mother, look to her and
nurse; the eagle, raven, owl, bat, whale and dolphin are ready to drop in; animals call by what they are doing (a snort when
alarmed, a huff when warning, a roar only on the charge); and eye shine shows by moonlight too, as two points far beyond where you can
see the body.

**PR 10, animal sign.** Animals leave tracks on soft ground (mud, sand, snow, wet grass) one print per step, in groups of four when
they run and a Y for a running rabbit; and droppings, nibbled plants and flattened beds where they fed and slept. A tracker can tell
the animal, its direction, how old the trail is and whether it was running.

**PR 11, weather looks.** Dogs and wolves shake themselves when the rain stops; grazers turn their rumps to a gale; herds huddle with
heads low in hard frost; breath steams below 5 C; snow builds on a sleeping animal's back.

**PR 12, hunting feedback.** A struck animal reacts to where it was hit (a head toss, a stumble, a buckled leg), drags a hind leg
after a hip hit, limps when badly hurt, and falls on its side along the slope instead of vanishing.

**PR 13, the cost pass and review.** Nothing new to see; the animal looks cost less, and one switch turns them all off for a test.

**PR 14, follow-ups.** Air temperature by height, Jamie's track spacing, animals standing hunched and still in shelter, the hunting
fields Steve sends, and Dino's picture list.

**PR 15, posed birds.** When your Meshy eagle, raven, owl and bat arrive, they flap, glide, fold their wings and flare to land from
their own skeletons, with no animation clips needed.

**PR 16, posed swimmers.** Your Meshy whale and dolphin swim with their tails beating up and down from their own spines, and the
dolphin leaps in arcs.

**PR 17, wind direction.** In wind and rain, grazers stand with their backs to it, heads down; deer graze into a breeze; fur lifts on
the windward side; breath drifts downwind; birds take off into the wind.

**PR 18, fog.** In fog, eye shine shows from dusk but only to a third of what you can see; breath blends into the mist; herds keep
closer together.

**PR 19, prevailing wind.** The wind animals react to is the weather's own, so it follows each part of the island (west-north-west at the starter); birds' feathers ruffle; an
alarmed animal turns its nose into the wind to smell what scared it.

**PR 20, cave air.** Deep in a cave an animal's breath does not steam (the cave is 5.5 C whatever the sky); coats stop drying
underground.

**PR 21, drinking.** Animals drinking lift their heads to look round between sips, used drinking spots get a trampled, churned bank,
and birds bathe in the shallows with splashes.

**PR 22, ice and snow.** Deer slip on frozen ponds while wolves cross steadily; snow builds on a still animal's back and melts when it
warms; prints in deep snow are bigger.

**PR 23, thunder.** At a clap of thunder birds start up flapping, deer and cattle flinch and bunch together with ears flat for a few
seconds, and your dog cowers.

**PR 24, pose library.** An animal whose model has no animation for sitting, lying, sheltering hunched, drinking or sleeping is posed from
its own skeleton instead: head down to the water when it drinks, legs folded when it lies.

**PR 25, breath and steam.** On a frosty morning every animal's breath shows, slow and soft when it rests, fast and heavy after it has
run; an animal that has swum or stood out the rain, or just made a kill, steams off its back.
