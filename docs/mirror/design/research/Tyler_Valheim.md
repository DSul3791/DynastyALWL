# Valheim: research for DynastyALWL's early-game flow (Tyler, 24 Oct 2026)
Sources: [Mobalytics early progression guide](https://mobalytics.gg/gamebase/guides/valheim-early-progression-guide), [XGamingServer base building guide](https://xgamingserver.com/blog/valheim-base-building-guide/), [Vaporlens review summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/892970/valheim.md). Facts below come from these pages; the opinions are mine and marked.

## How the first hours work
- Gather wood and stone, craft a hammer, place a workbench. Most building pieces need a workbench nearby; a workbench also stops monsters spawning inside its radius.
- First weapons are flint (found on riversides and shores): flint axe and flint spear. A chopping block next to the workbench raises its level and unlocks upgrades.
- Shelter: a small wooden house with a roof gap for a campfire's smoke (smoke hurts you otherwise), a bed (sets your spawn), storage.
- "Rested" buff: health regeneration +50 percent, stamina regeneration +100 percent; its length depends on COMFORT, which rises with each different comfort item near a fire (bed, chair, table, banner, rug). This turns decorating into a gameplay reason.
- Food: cooked meat and honey early; food gives health and stamina for a time.
- Tool tiers are gated by biome and boss: flint, then antler pickaxe (10 wood + 1 hard antler, a boss drop) for copper and tin, then bronze (2 copper + 1 tin at a forge, forge needs 6 copper, 4 stone, 4 coal, 10 wood), then iron from scrap in the swamp (needed for the iron pickaxe, which unlocks silver).
- Building shows structural integrity as a colour on each piece (blue strongest, red about to fall).

## What it does best
1. A clear next goal at every moment: each tier points to the next biome and the next resource.
2. Shelter and decoration matter mechanically (Rested/Comfort), not only visually.
3. The building system is praised as one of the best in the genre; the colour overlay teaches physics without a manual.
4. Co-op: players rate it best with friends; the biomes give each area its own look, enemies and resources.

## What to copy
- Soft-ore first pickaxe: the first pickaxe is NOT stone and NOT iron; it is a bone or antler pick for the soft metals (copper, tin). Answer to Dave's open question: stone cannot break iron, so the first pick is bone/antler/flint-tipped for copper and tin, bronze comes next, and iron needs a bronze pick (my adaptation, the tier order is Valheim's).
- Comfort and Rested tied to bed, fire and furniture; our rough bed becomes the base level (rest, hunger and thirst slow, safe).
- Colour overlay for building integrity (hover with the hammer).
- One gate per tier, shown to the player.

## What to avoid (from reviews)
- Skill loss on death feels punishing and spirals. Hauling ore by hand (portals cannot carry metal) is tedious. Late-game grind for rare resources. No equipment slots, so gear fills the inventory; stamina is often called frustrating. Fog and rough terrain in the last biome (hard to read).
- It teaches little: new players do not learn what to do first. Our inner voice hints ("I saw a stick on the ground", "if I had twine I could combine this rock with a stick") fix exactly that.

## Pitch: why Valheim's way is the best (5 lines)
1. Every tier has one clear goal and one gate (a biome plus a boss), so the player always knows the next step.
2. Shelter pays you back (Rested, Comfort), so building a home and decorating it is useful, not only pretty.
3. Fire, roof and bed teach survival by doing, with smoke and rain as simple rules.
4. The first pickaxe being soft-metal only (antler) solves the "rock cannot break iron" problem with a believable chain: flint, antler, bronze, iron.
5. Building with a visible integrity colour makes big houses safe to try; combine it with our inner voice and a gentler death penalty for the best of both.
