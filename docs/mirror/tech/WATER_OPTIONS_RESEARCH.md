# Water options research: DRAFT NOTES (Guru, 2026-10-23 04:45 UTC; Dave asked 04:33; finish tomorrow)
Status: partial. Web searches done, nothing built. Not yet asked Steve and Sonny for code facts.
## What we have (from our own docs)
Native C++ water core (water_core), cell-based, voxel-mutable (digs and fills change it), runs on one server water thread capped at 50% of a core. Measured (WATER_COST_REPORT.md, 2026-10-21, cloud core): 5.6 ms per tick at x10 flow, 21.6 ms at x20 storm, 32 ms at x20 with digs; 7x to 46x faster than game time under the cap; about 125 MB per tile of water memory (375 MB for 3 tiles). Draw: client builds water chunks (12 cm draw threshold), plus a far-water flat layer (cheap picture beyond draw range), custom shader (water_material.gd, depth-buffer thickness).
## Options found
1. KEEP OUR SIM, FIX THE SHADER. Our sim is the same kind of cellular automaton other voxel games use (DwarfCorp: each cell stores a level 0-255 and flow vector, falls down first then spreads sideways, evaporates below a threshold; no pressure, so water never flows upward; source: gamedeveloper.com "how water works in dwarfcorp"). Ours already does more (rivers, tile borders, digging).
2. Godot water RENDERING add-ons (do not simulate): Waterways (Arnklit WaterGenGodot, MIT): river meshes from bezier curves with baked flow and foam maps, buoyancy helper; limits per its README: rivers only (no lakes or oceans), no real-time reflections, cannot merge or split rivers; not voxel-mutable. Also Boujie Water Shader, OceanPro (GPU FFT ocean for Godot 4.7, forum), godotshaders water shaders. Good for a nicer SURFACE look only; FFT oceans cost GPU (our floor is a GTX 1050).
3. Virtual pipes / shallow-water style: Contraptioneer (Godot, voxel liquids 1 m) runs a small virtual-pipes sim per horizontal slice only to make a velocity (flow) map for the shader, caches it per vertex, rebuilds a chunk's mesh only when its shape changes, meshes liquids on a separate thread (engineer-game.org devlog 2025-12-22). Useful idea for us: flow-direction ripples in the shader from our own sim's flow, not a new sim.
4. Heightfield GPU erosion/shallow water (papers: Fast Hydraulic and Thermal Erosion on GPU, EG 2011, and others): good for BAKING terrain and rivers (terrain B basins) offline, not for live editable water on a headless server.
5. SPH / particle fluids: far too costly for an MMO server and for weak GPUs; not recommended (no source checked).
6. Zylann godot_voxel (we compile Voxel Tools 1.7): the docs list VoxelBlockyFluid and VoxelBlockyModelFluid classes (blocky fluid MESHING of liquid voxels). I could NOT verify from the docs whether any liquid SIMULATION exists (the pages I fetched only list the class names). Do not assume it simulates.
## Early recommendation (to confirm tomorrow with Steve and Sonny)
Keep our own sim (it is built, measured, voxel-mutable, saves, server-cheap). Do not adopt a different simulation. Look at two cheap rendering ideas instead: (a) flow-direction ripples from our sim's flow like Contraptioneer's cached flow map, (b) checking VoxelBlockyModelFluid meshing only if the shader flicker (Dave's water surface texture bug) cannot be fixed in our own shader. Dave's flicker is a surface texture/layering bug, not a simulation problem, so a new sim would not fix it.
