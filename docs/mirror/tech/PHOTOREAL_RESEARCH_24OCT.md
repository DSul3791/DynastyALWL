# From blocky to photoreal in Godot 4 on a budget (Tyler, 24 Oct 2026; for Kathleen via the coordinator)
Verified = I read the page. Background = from my own knowledge, check before relying.

## What Dave's goal needs, in order of visual payoff per cost
1. **Ground and rock materials (biggest win, cheapest).** Replace flat colours with PBR sets (albedo, normal, roughness, ambient occlusion, height) and blend them by terrain type.
   - Free libraries: Poly Haven (CC0, any use incl. commercial, no attribution needed; verified https://docs.polyhaven.com/en/faq), ambientCG (CC0, same idea; background; https://ambientcg.com). Both also give HDRI skies.
   - Megascans via Fab: free "all engines" only through end of 2024; since 2025 most assets are paid, individually priced from $0.99 (verified https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/10/epic-games-has-made-megascans-free-to-all-but-only-until-the-end-of-2024/). Not the first stop on a budget.
2. **Voxel terrain texturing.** Zylann's Voxel Tools terrain takes a ShaderMaterial. Community practice: world-space triplanar mapping so no UVs are needed; on its own it tiles visibly, so layer a large low-frequency texture over the high-frequency detail texture (verified https://forum.godotengine.org/t/texture-questions-with-zylanns-voxel-module/139597). For several surface types the standard approach is a texture array chosen per vertex from voxel data (background; see the docs https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/ and the TokisanGames voxelgame demo https://github.com/TokisanGames/voxelgame). Add slope-based blending (rock on steep, grass on flat, sand near water) and distance-fade of detail.
3. **Vegetation.** Grass, shrubs and trees carry most of the "alive" look. Free workflow shown at GodotFest 2025 (talk by Karl Bittner, CC BY 4.0, verified https://media.ccc.de/v/godotfest2025-plants-polygons-and-pixels-large-scale-vegetation-rendering-in-godot): EZTree and Blender for trees, billboard plants from photographs, impostors/LOD for distance, placement from geographic data. Our scatter already is placed by data, so the new cost is meshes, alpha-cut leaf cards with translucency, wind shader, and LOD with impostors.
4. **Lighting and post (a day of settings, big result).** Forward+ renderer: SDFGI for bounce light, SSAO/SSIL, glow, volumetric fog for god rays and distance haze, filmic/ACES tonemapping, a physical sky from an HDRI, soft cascaded shadows (docs: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html). Cost: GPU time; needs quality tiers so Dave's PC and laptops can turn them down. Check against our Mobile/Compatibility renderer choice (we run through the box with lavapipe; Forward+ needs Vulkan).
5. **Scanned or modelled assets.** Photogrammetry kits cost money (Megascans paid, Sketchfab/Quixel-style libraries vary by licence: read each one). Cheaper: Poly Haven models (CC0), Blender-built props, Kenney-style free kits for blocking.
6. **AI-assisted textures.** Useful for variants and breaking repetition (tileable generators, image-to-PBR map tools). Rule for us: licence of the generator output must allow commercial use, keep the prompt/source in git, and every texture goes through the same seamless/tiling and PBR-map checks. Background only; I did not verify any tool's licence.

## Terrain, tiles and the water: what stays safe
Only materials and meshes change; the 1921-sample tiles, shared edges and bake stay as they are. Texturing happens in the shader from world position and voxel/slope data, so no tile rebake is needed for look changes. Water needs its own material (refraction, foam, flow normal map), separate from terrain.

## Recommended first step (Kathleen, one branch, RUNTIME, client only)
**Terrain material pass on the ground only**: 4 to 6 CC0 PBR sets (grass, dirt/mud, rock, sand, snow/gravel) from Poly Haven or ambientCG, world-space triplanar + large-scale breakup layer + slope blend, distance fade; no new geometry, no bake.
