# DAVE'S RULE (2026-10-21): waterfalls are NEVER steps or terraces. One clean chute per drop. If a cliff lands on a plateau: plunge pool, then a river across the plateau to the next waterfall, another clean chute with its own pool. This overrides any 'uneven cascade steps' advice below or in FINDINGS.md (retracted).

# Best practices by topic (Tyler). Ask me before a NEW kind of job; if your topic is listed, you are covered. Newest first; each entry: rule, source, owner.

## Waterfalls (water look and shape)
- Faces stay flat and vertical over a hard lip; add a plunge pool and a boulder apron; NO cascade steps (Dave): one chute per drop, pool, river, next chute. See FINDINGS.md "fall shape". Sources: Lamb and Dietrich (Caltech), Scheingross flume study.
- Water: curved ballistic throw off the lip, frays into streams at normal flow, one thick aerated sheet in storms, foam ring plus particle mist at landing (mist scales with height x flow).

## Channel edges (rivers and brooks)
- Carve from smooth distance-to-centreline, not cell flags; blend soil bands with continuous depth plus noise; never threshold into stripes. See FINDINGS.md P0.

## Terrain erosion and mountains
- Thermal erosion first (talus 30 to 40 degrees), then hydraulic, masked so cliffs stay cliffs; vary rock bed thickness and add slight dip. Sources: Hesiod and World Machine docs. Any change to tiles needs Steve's bake slot.

## Sky and distance
- Aerial perspective (haze that takes the sky colour) before anything fancier; keep an ambient floor at night and in storms. Source: Hillaire 2020.

## Trees and plants (mid LOD, far shadow proxy, impostors, in-chunk merge already built; see FINDINGS Report 3 correction)
- Soft leaf cards with outward-pointing normals, per-card colour jitter; keep MultiMesh per species and impostors; do not add per-tree nodes.

## Fall generation push (given to Sonny 2026-10-21)
Order: thermal erosion, then hydraulic (prototype only, hillshade first), rock beds, rivers and falls at hard-bed crossings, plunge pool and apron, soil last. Beds log-normal (sigma 0.4, 0.4x-2.5x), dip 1-5 deg, bench tops 1-3 deg. (Cascade step numbers RETRACTED: no steps.) Lip notch 20-60 cm is fine. Pool depth 0.3-0.5 H (cap 4 m), radius 0.4-0.7 H. Apron 8-25 angular half-buried blocks 0.3-2 m within 1.5 H. Judge by hillshade crop, lip-to-pool cross-section and side-on stills at normal and 20x flow. Avoid equal steps, round pools, boulder rows.

## Survival mechanics (given to Radar 2026-10-21)
Spoilage by lazy timestamp (made_at + rate, computed on read), never per-stack ticks; separate stacks by freshness or merge by weighted age. Cold from clock, zone, altitude, clothing, shelter, fire on a slow server tick (1-5 s) with hysteresis; ramped damage. Remains save and expire server-side, capped per player, tested across a restart. All rolls through luck.gd; old saves load with neutral defaults (test).

## River flow rates and lake surface (given to Sonny 2026-10-21)
Mean Q: brook 0.05-0.5 m3/s, small river 1-10, medium 10-100, large 100-1000+. Speeds 0.3-1.2 m/s lowland, 1-3 mountain, rapids 4-5. Width ~ Q^0.5 (coefficient ~3-5, unverified; Hey and Thorne 1986, Parker et al 2007). Lakes: mirror-flat, small wind ripples only, no standing peaks; clamp/damp the sim in still-water cells; flow boost only on channel cells; test 1x vs 10x with stills and a 10 s time-lapse.

## Skills system (given to Radar 2026-10-21)
Diminishing gain as skill rises, scaled by task difficulty, none for trivial repeats; per-skill cooldown plus daily soft cap, count only server-verified successes; smooth saturating curve (effective = base + (1-base)*(1-exp(-k*practice))) tuned by 1-2 numbers in JSON; lazy decay on load from last_used with 20% floor, shown as "rusty" on Tab; gains via luck.gd; tests: 10,000 identical clicks reach the cap not infinity, old saves load with zeros.

## Cooking (given to Radar 2026-10-21; from general food-safety knowledge, ranges, not sourced)
Keeping without fridge, cool weather: raw meat/fish ~1 day, cooked meat/stew 1-3 days (1 day warm), porridge 1-2, bread 3-5, cooked veg 2-4; cooked = ~2-3x raw meat, never beyond naturally durable roots/nuts. Drying, smoking, salting is the real long keeping (later job); a pot re-boiled daily keeps longer. Warmth: small short boost, drinks bigger than solids, stew best. Not safe raw: meat, fish, eggs, fiddleheads, kidney/dried beans, acorns (leach), green potatoes, wild mushrooms. Wrong-raw = mild sickness, tag "cook me" in data.

## Daily temperature swing (given to Radar 2026-10-21; climatology rules of thumb)
Low ~30-60 min after sunrise, high ~14-16 h solar, skewed curve. Clear-calm swing: temperate summer 10-12 C, spring/autumn 8-10, winter 4-6; transition 7-13; desert 16-22 (25 dry); arctic summer 6-9, winter 1-3. ## Ice fishing, spear fishing, bench tier 2 (given to Radar 2026-10-21; published rules of thumb)

## Erosion parameters for the generator push (given to Willy, Sonny, Steve 2026-10-21; standard values from Hesiod/World Machine docs and the common droplet model, tune by hillshade)
Order inside the existing pipeline: thermal, then hydraulic, then rock beds/ledges (tile_height.gd already defers ledges until after erosion), then river routing and falls, then pools/apron, then soil. Thermal: talus tan(33 deg) per cell (30-38), move 50% of the excess, 20-60 iterations, skip cells steeper than 50 deg where a hard bed (ledge > 0) stands (cliffs stay cliffs). Hydraulic droplets: inertia 0.05, capacity 4, erosion 0.3, deposition 0.3, evaporation 0.01, gravity 4, lifetime 30 steps, brush radius 3 cells, 2-4 droplets per cell; erodibility = soil 1.0, soft rock 0.6, hard bed 0.2; clamp net change to 6 m per cell. Masks (fade to 0): 60 m around river/pond/fall paths and lips, 300 m around spawn, sea cells and flats under 3 deg, 5 cells at tile edges unless run on a 3x3 halo with global coordinates (then no edge mask, deterministic seed from coordinates). Judge: hillshade before/after, max delta map, volume balance within a few percent, no change inside masks (assert).

## Animals and hunting (for Radar, Steve, Creature thread; 2026-10-21)
Sources: AoI grid, spawn/despawn hysteresis and offline catch-up patterns ([coherence MMO docs](https://docs.coherence.io/1.2/learning-coherence/how-to-network-x/how-to-mmo), [AoI write-up](https://mintlify.wiki/kenzz55/ue5-iocp-mmo-server/systems/aoi), [spawn devlog](https://birdmaskstudio.itch.io/days-with-dino/devlog/245258/thinking-about-mob-spawning-in-a-open-world-22nd-april-2021-development-notes), [offline catch-up thread](https://makegamessa.com/discussion/comment/36174)); survival-game behaviour from player feedback and mods (flee that flips to fight when cornered, prey stamina, need-driven hunting, alarm spreading to nearby herd members: [Vintage Story](https://www.vintagestory.at/forums/topic/11784-long-range-attacks-should-make-animals-flee-or-aggro/), [Rust request](https://umod.org/community/plugin-requests/7646-animal-aggression-without-retreat-rust)). Numbers below are my design recommendations, not sourced.
ARCHITECTURE: server owns animals; no navmesh, steer on the heightfield (slope limit, water avoid); simulate only inside an active radius (~200 m) of any player; outside that, animals are dormant: store population per 128 m cell (count, species, last_seen time) and catch up on return (spawn the expected number, capped). Spawn out of sight at 150-250 m, despawn beyond ~350 m (hysteresis); cap spawns per tick per zone; spawn never within 60 m of a player and never in view.
BUDGET: 20 players: server animal CPU <= 2 ms per tick total; <= ~30 active animals per player, ~150-300 worldwide; AI tick by distance: <60 m 10 Hz, 60-150 m 3 Hz, 150-200 m 0.5 Hz; network: only animals inside the AoI, positions quantised, client interpolates; client render budget like characters (1 ms for ~20 visible at Low, distance cull, animation LOD); no per-animal physics bodies except nearby (< 40 m).
BEHAVIOUR (small state machine): graze/idle, alert (detection), flee, regroup (herd), hunt (predators only when hungry), cornered/low health flips flee to fight. Senses: sight cone, hearing radius scaled by noise (sprint, chop, dig, fighting), smell via the existing wind_dir so downwind approach works; alarm spreads to same-species neighbours within ~40 m; prey have stamina and tire, predators give up when they tire.
ECONOMY: carcass entity decays, capped per tile; population per cell regrows logistically (slow, about a game week to refill) so overhunting hurts; yields via luck.gd and carcass.json; hunting skill via skills system; raw meat raw_unsafe; weapons: spear first, bow later.
PROCESS: first push = one species (rabbit or deer) with the AoI and catch-up machinery and an admin spawn command and a stress test with 20 simulated players; measure server ms before adding species.

## NPCs: swappable AI/static dialogue, schedules (for Radar, Steve; 2026-10-21)
Sources: latency tiers, mandatory fallback, streaming, token budgets per NPC, "model phrases, state machine owns facts", treat player text as hostile ([AI NPC system guide](https://www.respan.ai/resources/building-ai-npc-system), [best practices](https://aischool.lillytechsystems.com/ai-npc-characters/best-practices.html), [fixed-persona SLM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10277)); schedules and packages ([Radiant AI](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Radiant_A_I), [Creation Kit packages](https://ck.uesp.net/wiki/Bethesda_Tutorial_Packages)). Thin sources on memory and injection testing: the numbers below are my recommendations.
ARCHITECTURE: one interface, DialogueProvider.get_line(npc_id, context, player_input) -> {text, source, voice_id, actions}. Two providers: StaticProvider (always available, data JSON) and AIProvider (async worker, never on the server tick). A Router picks: AI if healthy, else Static. Server-authoritative state (quests, items, schedule, relationships) is the truth; the AI only phrases lines and may return actions only from a whitelist validated by the server (never free commands, never item grants it was not given).
FALLBACK / HEALTH: per-call timeout (first token 1.5 s, total 4 s; show a short "thinking" idle, never freeze the player), circuit breaker (3 failures in 30 s opens it for 60 s; a cheap health probe every 30 s closes it), instant switch both ways, same voice_id and NPC identity either way, log which provider answered. Dave's fallback ("unwell" NPC): a static line like "I apologize, I have had a headache all day", then at most 1-2 short info/instruction lines drawn from quest data, then the NPC ends the talk and walks to bed or chair; talk cooldown 2-5 game minutes; no repeat of a line within the last 5; flag the NPC as unwell in the world (idle animation, sound) so players read it as in-world, not a bug.
SAFETY AND COST: input cap (200 chars), rate limit (1 message per 3 s per player), per-player daily token budget and per-NPC session budget, moderate input and output, persona and rules only server-side, no secrets or hidden quest answers in prompts beyond what the NPC knows, refuse out-of-world topics in character, call the AI only when a player addresses the NPC (no ambient chatter). Cost sketch: 20 active players at 6 messages/min = 2 calls/s; at ~600 tokens each that is ~4 M tokens/hour: use a small model, cache common replies, cap memory prompt to ~300 tokens. Queue limits so a burst cannot starve the server.
MEMORY: per NPC per player: facts as flags (met, helped, owes), a rolling summary <= ~300 tokens refreshed every ~10 turns, the last ~6 turns; store server-side, versioned, with a cap and decay; shared world facts come from game state not the chat log. Same flags drive the static provider, so switching keeps continuity.
STATIC DIALOGUE: data trees: nodes {id, text, conditions, choices, effects}, plus barks (greeting, farewell, idle) and topics keyed to quest and item data; localisable ids; tests that every node is reachable and every effect is valid.
SCHEDULES: Radiant-style packages: one active package per NPC (sleep, sit, work, eat, wander) chosen from a 24-hour table (hourly slots, data), executed at world markers (bed, chair, workbench) that have slots so two NPCs never take one chair; add +-10 min jitter per NPC so a town does not move at once (famous Oblivion lunch-tavern bug); dormant NPCs far from players are not simulated: on activation place them at the marker their schedule says; interrupt a package for talk and resume it after. Weather and night can override (shelter in rain).

## Game TTS: NPC and inner voice (for Mandy, Radar, Steve; 2026-10-21)
Sources: Godot TTS uses the player's OS voices, differs per PC, and returns no voices on Linux without speech libraries ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/tutorials/audio/text_to_speech.html), [itch.io write-up](https://itch.io/blog/725034/tts-in-godot-advice-and-current-limitations.amp)); licences from secondary sources, not the licence texts ([comparison](https://aivideosensei.com/compare/kokoro-vs-chatterbox-vs-xtts), [licence guide](https://medium.com/@warisruzi/choosing-the-right-license-for-text-to-speech-what-every-builder-should-know-d72eb212b421)). Not legal advice: read each licence and voice card.
1 CHOICE: pre-generate audio offline (files like inner_voice/<line_id>.ogg, already supported) rather than DisplayServer.tts_speak: identical on every PC, works on Linux, loudness and effects under our control, no async timing. Keep OS TTS only as an optional accessibility reader. For AI NPCs later, use the SAME engine and voice id at runtime on the server (streaming per sentence), so static and AI lines sound like one character: this favours an engine that runs both batch and live on CPU.
2 LICENCES: Kokoro is Apache 2.0 (commercial use fine, fixed ~54 presets, no cloning). Piper: the old repo is MIT, the maintained one is reported GPL-3.0 (embeds eSpeak NG) and each voice has its own dataset licence (CC0, CC-BY, some restricted); check every voice card and keep a LICENSES manifest. Coqui XTTS weights are non-commercial (CPML) and its company is gone: do not ship. Generated audio is generally not a derivative of the engine code, but confirm per voice card. Do not clone a real person's voice without written consent.
3 WHISPER-IN-THE-HEADSET (Inner bus, Godot effects): high-pass 150 Hz, gentle low-pass 6-7 kHz, compressor (ratio ~3), small reverb (room 0.3, wet 12-18%), StereoEnhance or a 10-20 ms Haas delay so it feels inside the head, 1-2 dB under the world mix and ducked when the player speaks; slightly lower pitch/slower for the inner voice.
4 COST/SIZE: mono OGG Vorbis at ~24-32 kbps is ~3-4 KB per second: a 5 s line is ~15-20 KB, 1,000 lines ~15-20 MB. Piper/Kokoro-class models render faster than real time on a CPU, so batch generation is minutes, live per-sentence generation is feasible on a server core. Keep lines <= 12 words.
5 LESS ROBOTIC: split into short sentences and tune commas/ellipses for pauses; slow slightly (length_scale 1.05-1.15); random +-3% rate and pitch per line; text normalisation (numbers, abbreviations) and a lexicon for fantasy names; trim silences, normalise to about -18 LUFS; add faint room tone and breath; per-character speaker id plus a fixed pitch offset; avoid long paragraphs and all-caps; regenerate when the line text changes (hash the text in the filename).

## Skill web, training and progression curves (for Radar; Dave's redesign, 2026-10-21)
Evidence: Wurm (0-100 scale, no levels, little loss), Mortal Online (point budget, secondary skills learned from books and tutors), RimWorld (fixed trainer XP has diminishing returns, low skill gains more) ([Wurm](https://old.wurmonline.com/?p=2114), [Mortal Online](https://mortalonline.fandom.com/wiki/File:AttributesAndSkillsOverview.jpg), [RimWorld skill trainer](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Skilltrainer)); RuneScape XP: geometric, each level gap about 1.10x the last, total doubles about every 7 levels, but higher activities pay more XP so wall-clock is gentler ([OSRS XP table](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Experience_table)). Everything numeric below is my tuning recommendation: put it in JSON and simulate with a script before Dave sees it.
STRUCTURE: a directed graph (web), not a tree: nodes = skills; edges = parent (is-a: Fighting > Swordplay > Rapier) and links (uses attribute: Rapier uses Athleticism and Dexterity, Broadsword Athleticism and Strength). Roots = traits/attributes (Strength, Dexterity, Fitness, Luck, plus Intellect and Perception later); every skill traces to a trait. Data: id, parents, attribute weights, tier, training_requirements, xp_cost_scale; validated at load (no cycles, every node reaches a trait).
SCALE: show 1-100, everyone starts at 1. Caps by training level: 25 untrained, 50, 75, 100 (+25 per level, four levels; Dave's example used 50 with two, bigger scale keeps late play interesting). Subskill unlock thresholds on the parent: 15, 40, 65, 90 (tier 1 unlocks inside the untrained range, so even untrained players see progress). Training jump: finishing a level raises the family floor to the old cap + 5 (30, 55, 80) and the cap by 25; the floor applies to the skill and all its descendants (descendants only, not parents) via `effective = max(practice_level, floor)`, so no fan-out writes and decay never goes below it. Training level k requires practice level >= cap(k-1) - 5 (use it first, then get taught).
XP CURVE: cost to go from level n to n+1 = base x g^(n-1) with base ~40 practice units and g ~1.045 (RuneScape's 1.10 is too steep for a 100 scale): cumulative about 1.8 k units to 25, 7.1 k to 50, 23 k to 75, 68 k to 100. One practice unit = one successful, on-level action (per-skill cooldown stops spam). At about 5 units/min of real play that is roughly 6 h, 24 h, 80 h and 230 h for one deep skill; it is long enough to matter but a single skill is never the whole game, and the web has dozens. Each training level gives x1.2 gain rate ("your basics are better") and higher-tier actions pay more units (so late levels are not pure grind). Gain per action scales with difficulty: nothing when the task is well below your skill (skill - task difficulty > 20), up to x1.5 for tasks above it. Diminishing but never zero for real use.
FEED: a practice unit goes 80% to the skill used, 20% shared up the parents with a 0.4 ratio per generation (20%, 8%, 3%), parents cost 1.5x per generation so broad skills lag, and parent gain can never exceed its cap. Dave's rapier example falls out: practising rapier raises Swordplay and Fighting a little, rapier most. Attributes: they modify effectiveness (weights per skill, about +-20% at the extremes of -10..+10) and learning speed (about +-10%), not the cap.
TRAINING (the social piece): teacher needs the skill >= target + 10 and a different account; lesson takes real time (about 20-30 min together) and costs materials; cooldown per skill per level (about one game week = 7 real hours); teacher gains a small practice bonus; NPC trainers go up to level 1 or 2 only, higher tiers need players: this supports the player economy and makes training itself the unlock. Guards: no alt-loop teaching (cooldown per pair), server verifies proximity and time.
DECAY: gentle: after about 14 real days idle, lose 1% of practice XP per real day, never below the training floor or 20% of peak practice; "muscle memory": regain lost levels at x3 until the old peak. Lazy: compute from last_used on load. (Most MMOs have no decay; if Dave wants none, drop it, nothing else depends on it.)
MIGRATION: schema_version in skills file; old file kept as .prev; dry-run script prints before/after for every character; map old values to the new scale preserving total practice (xp-equivalent); anyone above 25 is grandfathered: training level = ceil(value/25) - 1, floor set to match; unknown or missing = 1; test with the existing characters.json, then the persistence drill.
TRAPS: leaf-spam levelling parents (hence parent cost and the cooldown), caps with no visible goal (show the next unlock and what training is needed), reset exploits via decay, trainer scarcity stalling new players (NPC level 1 always available), and showing raw numbers: show named ranks (Novice to Master) plus the number in inspection.

## River mouth / estuary (decision for Dave's picture, 2026-10-21)
SEAMS I FOUND IN THE CODE (picture does not show the sea itself; the strip shown has a texture change from smooth to hatched ripples and ragged grid-cut edges with wide dark bank bands):
1 Surface level: water_grid._ease_into_sea draws the river sheet as a ramp rising 5% (0.05 m per m) from 0 m, so 4.5 m of rise over 90 m: a visibly sloping river meeting a flat sea plane. Real rivers near the sea are almost flat (gradient 0.01-0.1%) and, in the last km, are held flat by backwater from the sea.
2 Texture: the river shader moves ripples downstream at a speed from slope (sqrt law) in a fine pattern, the sea uses slow still ripples at scale 5 m: the pattern changes at the boundary. Colour is already blended by height above the sea (MOUTH_FROM_M 0.4 to MOUTH_TO_M 4.5, i.e. over ~90 m of the ramp).
3 Edges and banks: the sheet ends on the 8 m grid and the banks (dark bands) stop abruptly; the sea plane has no shoreline of its own, so there is a visible step between a narrow channel and open water.
4 Foam: the mouth_band only adds a thin foam line; real mouths show a brackish mixing front and breaking waves on the bar, not a line across the channel.
REAL-WORLD MODEL: estuaries are funnel-shaped (width grows roughly exponentially toward the sea), shallow and flat-surfaced (backwater), with the river flow slowing to near zero at the sea, sandbars at the mouth where waves break, and a freshwater plume that mixes into the sea over a few mouth widths, colour fading from brown or green-grey to sea colour with a soft curved front, deflected by the coast current. Deltas form instead of funnels on low-energy coasts.
THE FIX (decided), in two parts:
A RUNTIME (no bake, Mandy and Sonny): (1) flatten the drawn river surface over the last 200 m to 0 m + tiny slope (0.005 instead of 0.05) and extend SEA_EASE_CELLS to about 25 cells, clamped above ground; (2) in the water shader blend the ripple field by mouth factor: flow speed fades to 0 and the pattern scale/direction changes to the sea's over about 120 m, no pattern seam; (3) extend the colour/clarity blend over the same 120-200 m plus a plume: colour fades into the sea over 2-4 mouth widths seaward, noise-broken edge; (4) foam: soft band where flow meets still water and a faint breaking-wave line on the bar 20-40 m seaward, none across the channel; (5) the sea plane gets a soft alpha shoreline fade where it overlaps the river sheet.

## Rivers fading before the ocean, flicker-free shores, dry-spell trickles (for Sonny and Mandy, 2026-10-21; hydrology rules of thumb plus the draw rules seen in water_sim.gd)
WHY A RIVER SEEMS TO FADE BEFORE THE SEA (likely, from code; verify with a depth map): the live sim draws water only where depth >= WET_M 0.12 m (and 0.6 m on steep drops). Toward the mouth the channel widens and slows, so the same flow spreads thinner; depth drops under 12 cm and the sheet is simply not drawn, leaving a dry-looking gap before the sea. Real estuaries are the opposite: wide but held deep by the sea's backwater. Fixes: (a) the backwater and drowned-channel fix in "River mouth / estuary" (bed below 0 m near the mouth); (b) near the sea (within ~300 m) draw any water above ~2 cm with a soft depth-based alpha instead of a hard cut, so thin water reads as a translucent film running out; (c) never let the drawn river end before the sea plane: if the sim reports a gap shorter than 150 m between two wet cells, bridge it with the lowest level.
FLICKER-FREE SHORES (standard practice): hysteresis (a cell becomes wet at 0.12 m but dries only below 0.06 m), a short temporal low-pass on depth (exponential, tau 1-2 s) before thresholding, a minimum wet/dry hold of about 2 s per cell, and an edge drawn from a smooth depth field (bilinear on the fine ground heights) with a soft alpha ramp over the last 5-10 cm, so the shoreline moves as a smooth contour, never cell by cell; keep the foam line tied to the same smooth field. The current film rules (FALL_FILM_M 0.02, THIN_WET_M 0.02) are the right idea; apply them everywhere, not only on channels.
DRY SPELLS: real rivers keep a baseflow from groundwater: temperate dry-season flow is about 10-30% of the mean (Q95 roughly 10-25% of mean flow; very small streams can dry fully, big rivers never). So: (1) modulate spring and rain input smoothly by season (spring melt 1.5x, summer 0.3-0.5x, autumn 0.8x, winter in cold zones about 0.2x as ice) over days, never in steps, floor at 15% of mean for rivers with more than 5 m3/s mean and allow 0 only for brooks under 0.1 m3/s; (2) an inner low-flow channel: carve the bed with a 1-2 m wide, 0.2-0.4 m deep thalweg inside the main channel, so low water stays in a thin continuous thread on the deepest line with pale gravel bars either side; (3) riffle-pool sequence: pools every 5-7 channel widths stay as isolated puddles when flow stops (a real drought look); (4) wet-dry transition colour: dark wet margin, pale dry gravel, algae stain at the old water line; (5) the drawn width should follow the real depth continuously (no 8 m cell steps).

## Natural walk, jog, run: numbers for the ragdoll (for Mandy, Dave's request 2026-10-21)
Sources: only the walk-run transition at Froude number about 0.5 is sourced ([Kram et al. via PMC, Apollo Number paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2719915/), [Kram 1997 gait transition](https://sites.utexas.edu/neptune/wp-content/uploads/sites/6031/2020/07/jeb2085.pdf)); my search found no source for the other values, which are typical adult gait-lab ranges from memory: tune by eye, they are targets not laws.
Body: 1.8 m, leg length about 0.9 m. Transition speed = sqrt(0.5 x g x leg) = about 2.1 m/s (so 2.0-2.2 m/s is where walk becomes jog; a walk faster than that looks like race-walking).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speed m/s | 1.0-1.8 (typical 1.4) | 2.2-3.5 | 3.5-6.5 | 7+ |
| Cadence steps/min | 100-120 | 150-165 | 165-180 | 190+ |
| Step length m | 0.65-0.8 | 0.9-1.2 | 1.2-1.6 | 1.8-2.2 |
| Forward trunk lean | 1-3 deg | 4-7 | 8-12 | 15-20 |
| Vertical bob (peak to peak) | 3-5 cm | 6-8 cm | 8-10 cm | 10-12 cm |
| Hip swing (flexion/extension) | +25/-10 | +35/-15 | +45/-20 | +55/-25 |
| Knee peak (swing phase) | 60 | 90-100 | 105-120 | 125+ |
| Knee at foot strike/stance | 15-20 | 35-40 | 40-45 | 45 |
| Elbow bend | 15-30 (varies a little) | 60-80 | 80-95 | 90-100 |
| Shoulder arm swing | +-15-20 | +-30 | +-40-50 | +-60 |
| Pelvis rotation / shoulder counter-rotation | +-4 / +-6 | +-5 / +-9 | +-6 / +-12 | +-8 / +-15 |
| Stance share of cycle | 60% (double support) | 40% | 35% | 30% (flight phases) |
Rules of motion: the lean is a rotation of the whole spine/chest forward, increasing with speed and with acceleration (add 3-5 deg while speeding up, lean back 2-3 deg when stopping); bob has two peaks per stride (one per step) and the lowest point is at mid-stance for walk, the highest at mid-flight for run; arms swing opposite to the same-side leg; shoulders counter-rotate against the pelvis; head stays level (counter the bob and lean by rotating the neck/head back to look at the horizon); in run, a flight phase where both feet are off the ground for ~10-15% of the cycle (a tiny hop in the body height curve, not only a leg change); foot placement lands heel-first walking, mid/fore foot running, toe-off pushes the body up.
Stride and speed: do not just speed up the same clip. Blend clips by speed on a BlendSpace1D (idle 0, walk 1.4, jog 3.0, run 5.0 m/s) and scale playback only within each clip's range (walk 1.0-1.8, jog 2.5-3.5, run 4-6.5) so the step length grows with speed (about step length = 0.415 x height at a walk, growing to about 0.8-0.9 x height at a run) rather than the legs spinning faster. A jog clip is missing today (idle, walk, run only): add it.
Slopes: on an uphill lean the body forward by about the slope angle (cap 25 deg) and shorten the steps 10-15%; downhill lean back 3-6 deg, shorten the steps, bend the knees 10 deg more; cross-slope no change. Stairs/steps: knee lift to 60+ deg on the lead leg. Sideways strafe and backward: half stride length, no run clip (use the walk clip) and lean toward the move direction by 1-2 deg.

## Player-built buildings and blueprints (design note for DESIGN_IDEAS.md)
Sources of practice: Valheim, Rust, Ark, Conan Exiles, Medieval Engineers, Space Engineers blueprints.
1 Pieces, not free voxels. A building is a list of pieces (foundation, wall, floor, roof, door, window, stairs), each with a type, material tier, grid cell and rotation. Snap points (sockets) on each piece decide what can attach, which keeps buildings valid and cheap to store.
2 Server authority. Client sends "place piece X at cell Y"; server checks range, snap, overlap, terrain slope, claim ownership and materials, then adds it. Never trust client positions.
3 Structural integrity (Valheim style): each piece has support 0 to 100 from the ground, with material limits (wood 1 to 2 storeys on its own, stone more, pillars and arches add). Show it as a colour ramp on the ghost preview. This gives realism for free and limits towers.
4 Blueprint = the piece list with positions relative to an origin. Store as a compact JSON or binary string, so sharing is a copy and paste code and a save slot. Placing a blueprint creates ghost pieces; each piece costs the real materials and is built only when materials are delivered (like a construction site), or the builder pays up front.
5 Cost and effort: sum of piece costs, build time per piece, tool and skill gating (masonry, carpentry skills) tying into the skills web.
6 Land: claim by placing a claim stone or hearth; a radius or tile claim, with a piece limit per claim to stop server load, and decay for abandoned claims (time since owner last seen).
7 Persistence and network: save pieces per terrain tile as a delta list; replicate by interest radius, send pieces in batches, never as separate nodes per piece.
8 Draw cost: instance by piece type and material (MultiMesh), merge a whole building into one mesh when it is static and a player is far, LOD and cull interiors, no per-piece collision shapes (merge into a few boxes).
9 Terrain: foundation flattens the cell from a stored footprint (a terrain edit that the saved edits system already stores), and pieces stay on a coarse grid (say 1 m cells, 0.5 m height) so snaps always line up.
10 Look: medieval stone and iron or timber work, as Dave's rule; modular piece kit shares textures so a building reads as one style.
