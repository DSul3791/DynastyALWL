## P1: fall shape (geology)
- Real falls over flat-lying hard beds keep a vertical face because the jointed caprock fails by toppling blocks (Lamb and Dietrich, Caltech: https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20090904-111129199). So flat vertical faces with a hard lip, which we now have (1be1f5f), are correct.
- Under the lip there should usually be a softer bed that has eroded back, giving an overhang or recess and a broken-block apron, plus a plunge pool; undercutting pools appear when a fall is about 5x taller than the flow depth (flume study, Scheingross: https://hal-insu.archives-ouvertes.fr/insu-01309188). Our faces are bare flat planes with no recess, no fallen blocks and no pool. Suggest: the generator cuts a plunge pool (depth about 0.3 to 0.5x fall height, capped at 4 m) and scatters a boulder apron (reuse the boulder system) at the base, and makes the face slightly back-cut with horizontal bed lines. Effort: medium. Owner: River start (Sonny) with Willy.
- Cascades of 10 steps at equal height look machine made. Vary step height and spacing (0.5x to 2x), and let lips be uneven by a few tens of cm so water concentrates in notches. Effort: small.

## P1: fall water look
Current: one ribbon (water_falls.gd), shader streaks, 8 to 48 mist cards (fall_mist.gd, alpha 0.22 flat white quads).
Problems I expect from the code: a ribbon plus streaks is a flat decal; mist of 8 cards at Low will read as floating pillows; no foam ring where the sheet lands, no throw at the lip.
Better, in order of value per cost:
1. Nappe throw: water leaves the lip with horizontal speed and falls on a parabola, so the sheet curves out from the face and a gap of air shows between sheet and rock for any fall over about 3 m. Two or three polyline points computed ballistically (x = v t, y = g t^2 / 2) cost nothing extra. Source on nappe form: sheet falls like flow over a sharp-crested weir (patent text summarised in https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5537696).
2. Thin sheets break into streams and drops; big flows stay one coherent sheet and hit the pool whole (informal, cosmoquest forum; hydraulics literature on sheet breakup, e.g. https://iahr.org/library/info?pid=10454). So at normal flow the ribbon should fray into 3 to 5 sub-streams with gaps and flicker; at 20x storm it should be one thick, aerated, creamy sheet. We have only width scaling today.
3. Landing: foam ring plus a rising mist plume. The usual breakdown of a game fall is cascade, splash, white water, foam, mist (Roblox VFX guide, https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/use-case-tutorials/vfx/create-waterfalls). Replace the flat cards with GPU particles (about 64 at Low is cheap, one draw) with soft depth-faded alpha, and a foam disc shader on the pool that scrolls outwards. Mist amount scales with fall height times flow, not just flow (a Timberborn request makes the same point).
4. Wet rock: dark, glossy, moss on the splash zone is already in ground_material.gd; extend the wet band up the face beside the sheet by the spray height.
Effort: 1 small, 2 medium, 3 medium, 4 small. Owner: Water Works for 1 to 3, Art direction for tuning. All client-only, so no bake and no protocol. Water thread's cost rules apply (state verts and draws at Low).

## Mountains
**M1 (P1): the far hills are smooth grey mounds.** In lighting_after.png and water_low.png the hills behind the trees are rounded, uniform grey-white blobs with no gullies, no ridgelines, no ledges, no snow or scree. Real mountain silhouettes are made by drainage: ridges and gullies branching downhill. Standard pipeline is thermal erosion first (slopes settle to the talus angle, about 30 to 40 degrees, debris piles as scree at the base), then hydraulic erosion carving drainage, with masks so cliffs stay cliffs ([Hesiod erosion guide](https://hesioddoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guides/erosion/), [World Machine thermal erosion](https://help.world-machine.com/topic/device-thermalerosion/), survey [Cordonier 2016](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonier16CGF.pdf)). The generator has rock-bed ledges and an erosion step (tile_height.gd) but the result at distance is still blobby. Check: render a height-only top-down hillshade of tile (3,4)'s mountain area and look for branching drainage. If it is missing, add a hydraulic pass at coarse scale (the bake is offline, so a droplet erosion cost is free at runtime, but it changes tiles, so Dave's single bake rule applies: Steve decides when). Effort: large. Owner: Grounds Keeper (Willy), bake timing Steve.

**M2 (P1): ledges are too regular.** slope_cover_test.png shows evenly spaced, equally tall, dead-flat steps, like a staircase or rice terraces. Real strata vary in thickness and hardness, dip a few degrees, pinch out, and break along joints. Suggest bed thickness drawn from a log-normal spread, a gentle dip (1 to 5 degrees) and fault offsets, bench tops sloped slightly and cut by gullies where water crosses. Texture fix, cheap: darker lichen/stain bands on faces keyed to bed index and vertical streaks below benches. Effort: small to medium. Owner: Willy.

**M3 (P2): rock look.** Faces read as uniform grey tinted by slope. Add triplanar rock with large-scale colour variation by rock type (granite pale and blocky, shale dark and platy), cracks, and ambient occlusion in crevices; keep snow and scree as slope-masked layers (snow only on flat to moderate slopes above the snow line, scree wedges at the base of cliffs where slope is 30 to 38 degrees). Owner: Art direction. Effort: medium.

## Skies (day_cycle_sheet.png, sky_clouds_ultra.png)
Good: the colour sequence dawn to dusk to night is believable; sunset and dusk clouds read well; stars and night clouds are present.
**S1 (P1): hard horizon, no aerial perspective.** In every frame the ground ends at a razor-sharp line with a flat grey strip (sky_clouds_ultra.png), and the distant trees are as dark and contrasty as near ones. Real distance goes pale and bluish with a soft horizon haze that takes the sun colour at dusk. This is the single biggest "realism per cost" item: a per-fragment distance fog tied to the sky colour (Godot built-in fog with sun-scatter and aerial perspective, height fog slightly denser in valleys) costs almost nothing. Reference technique, heavier: [Hillaire 2020](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14050) (sky plus aerial perspective, no big LUTs; also [pl-sky](https://github.com/hoffstadt/pl-sky) for a readable implementation). A cheap fit for us is to bake just the aerial-perspective colour ramp per time of day. Effort: small. Owner: Art direction. Check Low cost: one fog term.
**S2 (P2): clouds near the horizon turn to speckle.** The cloud layer breaks into small dotted bits just above the horizon (aliasing, no foreshortening). Fade cloud contrast to the haze colour towards the horizon and use a larger low-frequency layer there. Effort: small.

# Report 3: trees and plants versus draw cost (2026-10-21)

Evidence: tech/RENDER_AUDIT.md (Dave's probes: Ultra forest trees 7.6 ms, trees beyond 100 m 3.8, tree shadows 5.5 or 3.5 later; the meadow frame is CPU bound; about 970 nodes and 1000 draws at Low before the cuts), art/lighting_after.png.
Already right (do not touch): MultiMesh per species, alpha scissor not blend, far impostors, no shadows from grass, 2 shadow cascades below Ultra, tree shadows only within 60 m.

**T1 (P0): split the shadow caster from the visible crown.** Shadows of leaf-card crowns are the biggest single tree cost and also look worst (cards self-shadow and show as hard flakes: Unreal forum report, https://answers.unrealengine.com/questions/546824/view.html). Industry fix is a simplified shadow proxy: the full crown draws with shadows off, a coarse crown (about 15 to 25 percent of the cards, or a few blobs) draws with SHADOWS_ONLY, and only inside the shadow range ([SPIE shadow-imposter paper](https://nanolithography.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12784/127842E/Unreal-engine-nanite-foliage-shadow-imposter/10.1117/12.2692451.full), [Simplygon billboard clouds for shadows](https://www.simplygon.com/features/vegetationoptimization)). Expected: shadow cost down 60 to 75 percent and softer, truer tree shadows. Cost: one extra MultiMesh per species per chunk inside 60 m, so apply it only at Medium and above and merge chunk MMs to offset (see T3). Effort: medium. Owner: Mandy.

**T2 (P1): LOD by screen size, with a mid mesh.** Switch on projected size, not metres, so FOV and resolution scaling do not change what Low or Ultra show ([LOD best practices](https://pulsegeek.com/articles/lod_setup_best_practices_detail_where_it_matters) summarised; same advice in Simplygon's). Godot's visibility ranges work on MultiMeshInstance3D, fade by alpha only, and are best with two LODs; use dither with separate materials to avoid sorting problems ([Godot visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html)). Three steps: full crown, mid crown (about 25 percent triangles, bigger cards), impostor (already built). The audit flags "trees beyond 100 m 3.8 ms": that is where the mid LOD pays. Effort: medium. Owner: Mandy.

**T3 (P1): fewer, bigger MultiMeshes.** About 200 flora MultiMeshes per chunk for a few thousand instances means draws, not triangles, bound the CPU. MultiMesh has no per-instance culling or LOD, so the answer is chunk size: merge to one MM per species per larger cell (for example 128 m), cull by cell distance, rebuild on a worker thread to avoid buffer stalls (matches the [Godot forum discussion](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/best-approach-for-rendering-a-procedurally-generated-forest-with-thousands-of-trees-in-godot-4-7/141770)). Target: under 150 flora draws at Low. Effort: medium. Owner: Mandy with Willy.

**T4 (P2): look at no extra cost.** Leaf cards with crown-centred normals, colour jitter per card, wind sway in the vertex shader only (no per-tree nodes), 4 to 6 card shapes per species so repeats are hidden. Self-shadow acne on cards: set normal bias for leaf material, not global bias. Check each hardwood still at low sun for the flake look. Effort: small. Owner: Mandy.

**T5 (P2): plants and shrubs.** Beyond 25 m cast no shadow (audit item 3 is right); beyond 80 m draw only a coarse billboard cloud per cell; keep edible-plant interaction targets as invisible small colliders only within 2 m so that interaction does not need the visible mesh.

Order of value per effort: T1, T3, T2, T4, T5. Note Mandy is already cutting draws and shadows: I will check with her whether T1 overlaps her plan before anyone builds it.

Tone: keep AgX/filmic so gold does not clip.

Use zone latitude to scale it. After sunset the zenith stays blue for ~40 min while the horizon glows.

Skip at Low. This single effect sells scale and wind more than any sky texture.
5 DRIFT: clouds move with the wind at altitude, about 1.5-2x the surface wind speed and turned 10-30 degrees clockwise (northern hemisphere) from the surface wind direction; high cirrus faster again. Use the existing wind and wind_dir plus these factors so the debris cue and the clouds agree.
6 CLOUD LOOK: sharp bright cumulus top edges, shaded grey-blue bases, a thin bright rim ("silver lining") facing the sun, edges fray by noise; avoid uniform soft blobs (seen at noon). Moon: bigger glow and halo at night, phases on the lunar cycle (29.5 game days) with brightness driving night ambient (full moon about 3x new moon), stars fading as the moon brightens; keep a floor for readability (done).
8 STORM: lightning as 0.1-0.2 s ambient pulses with a short flicker, thunder delay by distance (about 3 s per km: sound hook); rain curtains in the distance as dark vertical streaks under the cloud base; sky turns green-grey before big storms only if Dave wants it.

# Report 12 (2026-10-21): the branch "pins" through the crowns (tree_builder.gd, oak model read)
What the stills show: thin pale blue-grey sticks stick out of every hardwood crown (lake_noon, dusk, lighting stills). From the code (_oak_limb, tree_builder.gd:1541-1578):
1 Blunt ends: _branch(bark, mid, to, r * 0.8, r * 0.62, ...) ends each limb at 62% of its starting radius, a cut-off cylinder, not a taper to a point; the last segment of a limb reads as a stick with a flat end.
2 Tips outside the leaves: leaf clumps are placed at `to` only when `depth >= 1 and depth < last`, and at the terminal `depth >= last`; limbs that finish at depth 0, or whose tip is clamped back into the crown dome (`to = center + rel / sqrt(e)`), can end without a clump around the tip, and a clump of size crown_w * 0.26 at the tip is centred on it, so the last 0.5 to 1 m of the limb pokes out one side.
3 Colour: inner branches get the same light bark tint as the trunk and the light sky ambient, so on a dark crown they read as bright blue-grey spokes. Branches inside a crown should be darker and warmer.
Fixes (data in the builder, no draw cost):
- Taper the last segment of every leaf-bearing limb to 10-15% of its start radius (r1 = r * 0.15) so it ends in a point hidden in the foliage.
- End every terminal inside a clump: shorten the last segment by 15%, and place a clump (size 0.26 x crown) on every limb end including depth 0 limbs that have no children, with the tip inside 0.6 x the clump radius from its centre.
- Darken inside branches by depth into the crown: multiply the branch colour by lerp(1.0, 0.35, inside) where inside = 1 - (distance from the crown surface / crown radius) clamped, and shift toward warm brown; keep trunk and lowest limbs at full colour.
- Cut the spoke length: no limb should extend beyond 0.85 of the crown dome radius at its tip.
- Check the other hardwood models (_maple, _beech, _chestnut, _birch) for the same r1 and tip rules.
Owner: Mandy. Cost: none (mesh data).

## C. River surface problems (Dave: square shapes, up on the sides), with real cross-section numbers, for Sonny
Real rivers (hydraulic geometry, Leopold and Maddock; typical values, not site-specific). Bankfull discharge Q in m3/s: width w ~ 3.5 Q^0.5 m, mean depth d ~ 0.3 Q^0.4 m, mean speed v ~ 0.9 Q^0.1 m/s (rough).
- Q 1 (brook/small): w 3-5 m, d 0.3-0.5 m, v 0.5-1 m/s, width/depth 8-12.
- Q 10 (small river): w 10-14 m, d 0.8-1 m, v 1-1.4 m/s, width/depth about 12-15.
- Q 100 (medium river): w 30-40 m, d 2-3 m, v 1.5-2 m/s, width/depth 15-20.
Cross-section shape: straight reach is a shallow shallow trapezoid or parabola, maximum depth near the centre (thalweg), banks 1:2 to 1:1 slopes, bank-to-bed transition rounded. In bends it is asymmetric: deep pool and steep or undercut bank on the outside (slope 1:1 to vertical, depth 1.5-2x mean), gently sloping point bar on the inside (1:10 to 1:20, depth 0 to half the mean). Riffles at crossings between bends are shallow (0.5x mean) and fast; pools deep and slow (1.5-2x). Spacing of pools about 5-7 channel widths; meander wavelength about 10-14 widths; sinuosity 1.2-1.5 for lowland rivers.
Water surface: effectively FLAT ACROSS the channel at a given point. Superelevation in a bend is dh = v^2 w / (g r): v 1.2 m/s, w 12 m, r 60 m gives 0.03 m, i.e. 3 cm, invisible. Only waves, hydraulic jumps, standing waves in fast riffles (centimetres to a few decimetres) and a small meniscus lift at banks (millimetres) exist. So water "up on the sides" is not natural; it is an artefact. The surface slopes only down the stream (0.1-1% lowland, 1-5% brooks, more in cascades).
Likely causes in our code (from water_grid.gd constants; check each by looking at wireframe, not verified):
1 8 m sim cells with FINE_SUB 4 (2 m fine cells) give square edges where wet and dry cells meet; shorelines follow the cell grid instead of the terrain contour. Fix: compute the shoreline from the fine ground height at vertex or pixel level: depth = level - ground(x,z) sampled at high resolution (1 m or better via the ground texture), and draw the edge as the 0-depth contour (marching squares) with an alpha fade over depth 0.05-0.25 m. Shape then follows the real banks and is round.
2 Per-cell surface level taken from the cell's own water, so at the sides (shallow water over a sloping bank) levels jump or ride up with the bank. Fix: one level per cross-section: take the level along the centreline (thalweg) as a smooth curve down the river and have the whole width at that level (bank cells with ground above it stay dry, no per-cell minimum film). For a river this means the surface is a ribbon mesh: centreline polyline with water level, extruded sideways to where the level plane meets the ground, then smoothed. The ribbon removes grid artefacts entirely and is cheap (few thousand triangles per km).
3 FILM_M 0.04 and WET_M 0.12 thresholds: the film of 4 cm over the ground follows bank slope so it looks like water climbing the bank. Fix: never draw a film that follows the ground; either water at its own level or nothing; fade by depth in the shader (depth to ground from the depth buffer or from the ground texture), not by geometry.
4 Banks too gentle or stepped: the 8 m grid cannot make a 1:1 bank, so water spreads sideways in a wide flat triangle; at 20 m wide rivers the section is a wide bowl. Fix: cut the bed in the generator as a real cross-section (trapezoid or rounded V, depth by Q table above, banks 1:1.5 to 1:2) so the water edge is a sharp, narrow transition; pools on outside of bends.
5 Square look also comes from foam, mist or flow-texture masks that are sampled per cell: sample with bilinear or smoothstep, never nearest.
Check for Sonny: render a top-down height-coloured view of one river reach with the water level plane drawn on it at wireframe; measure surface heights at three points across a section (left bank, centre, right bank): they should differ by under 3 cm in straights, under 10 cm in bends.
Owner: Sonny with Willy (bed cross-section, bake needed for the generator part), runtime ribbon/contour is no-bake.

# Report 23 (2026-10-21): how a real 300 m drop looks (Dave's fall at x 53450 z 69341 is a very long ramp)
Facts (references: Angel Falls 979 m, Yosemite Falls 739 m in three drops, Gavarnie 422 m, Kjosfossen, Sutherland Falls 580 m, Wallaman 268 m; geomorphology of horsetail and plunge types, Report 4 and 5): a tall fall is either a single vertical free fall (plunge) or a very steep, mostly vertical cliff the water clings to (horsetail), never a staircase of 8 m steps; Dave's rule stays: NO steps.
What a 300 m fall actually looks like, top to bottom:
1 TOP: a lip with a smooth rounded crest; the stream narrows and accelerates, thickness 0.1-0.5 m for a small river (a few m3/s at about 6-8 m/s at the lip). Water leaves the rock in a smooth glassy sheet (the nappe) 0.5-2 m thick for 5-10 m, with slightly rippled edges.
2 UPPER THIRD: the sheet stays coherent, white and glassy with vertical streaks (flow lines); edges begin to fray into rope-like strands. # Report 24 (2026-10-21): making Kokoro NPC speech sound natural (Dave: sounds like AI, no natural pauses, monotone rate and tone)
Sources: https://deapi.ai/blog/kokoro-tts-guide-how-to-control-41-voices-with-nothing-but-punctuation , https://localaimaster.com/blog/kokoro-vs-xtts-vs-chatterbox , plus audio practice. I could not listen to the clips; numbers are starting values to tune by ear with Dave.
Why it sounds like AI: Kokoro reads each call as one even-paced run: constant rate, pauses only where punctuation tells it, identical sentence timing and no breaths or room. People vary pace phrase to phrase, pause for thought, and sound like they are in a place.
## 1 Control the timing yourself (biggest win, free)
Split every line into phrases at , ; : - ... . ? ! and synthesise each phrase as its own call (Kokoro prosody degrades past about 500 characters and resets intonation at a period anyway). Put the measured silence in yourself and join:
- comma 180-280 ms; semicolon or dash 250-350 ms; colon 300-400 ms; sentence end (. ? !) 450-700 ms; ellipsis 600-900 ms; paragraph or speaker beat 900-1200 ms; before a question or an "I" thought 100 ms extra.
- Randomise each pause by +-15% from a seed (hash of the text) so no two sound the same and repeats of the same line still match.
- Short stuff first: "Hm." / "Well..." as separate fragments give natural hesitations.
## 2 Per-phrase speed and pitch
Keep base speed per NPC (0.9-1.0; old, wise or tired 0.85-0.92; nervous or young 1.05-1.1) and vary each phrase +-4-6% from the seed: first phrase of a sentence 1.0, middle slightly faster (1.03), last phrase slower (0.94-0.96), asides and parentheticals faster and lower (pitch -1 semitone), emphasised phrase slower (0.9). Pitch (applied offline or by a pitch shifter, not resampling): mood offsets angry -1, sad -1.5 and slower, excited +1.5 and faster 1.08, sick 0.8 speed and breathy, whisper by voice choice. Keep within +-2 semitones or it sounds chipmunk or robotic.
## 3 Wording and punctuation the model responds to
Short sentences (6-12 words, as the data already requires), contractions ("I'd", "don't", "it's"), dashes for self-interruption ("Well - I suppose so."), ellipses for trailing off, commas where a speaker would breathe, "?" only on real questions, "!" once at most, stacked periods for punch ("Every. Single. Time."), no ALL CAPS (ignored), no emoji, SSML or [emotion] tags (read aloud or silence). Use IPA in square brackets only for pronunciation of fantasy names. Write lines as speech, not prose: fillers ("Ah.", "Hmm.", "Right then.") from a per-NPC filler list, used at most one per reply.
## 4 Voices and blending
Choose the more expressive voices: for English the top-rated Kokoro voices are af_heart, af_bella, af_nicole (female) and bm_fable, bm_george, bm_lewis, am_michael, am_fenrir (male); British (bm_/bf_) voices suit a medieval world; storyteller bm_fable. Blend two voices by a weighted average of their style vectors (kokoro-onnx accepts a numpy blend: 0.7 voiceA + 0.3 voiceB) to make many distinct NPCs from few voices, and vary the blend weight per NPC (store in the NPC data). Judge each voice on a 5-line test (a question, a threat, a joke, a sad line, an instruction) with Dave and drop the flat ones.
## 5 Light post-processing (same chain for every line)
High-pass 80 Hz; gentle cut of 1.5-2.5 dB around 3-4 kHz (reduces the synthetic edge); low shelf +1.5 dB at 200 Hz for warmth; soft compression 2:1 for even level; tiny room: short reverb (RT60 0.25-0.4 s indoors, 0.1 s outdoors, 6-10% wet) so voices sit in the space; trim and fade 10 ms; loudness -23 LUFS as before. Insert a quiet breath sample (-30 to -26 dB, 150-300 ms) at about one in three sentence gaps and before long lines, from 4-6 recorded breaths per voice gender: this does more for "human" than most processing. Do not add random pitch wobble or heavy reverb: it sounds worse.
## 6 The same path for prebaked and live client-side voice (MUST HOLD)
One shared module (render_voice_line) does: split to phrases, per-phrase speed and pitch, synthesise, insert measured pauses and breaths, concatenate. Inputs are only {voice or blend, base speed, mood, text, seed = hash(text)}. All randomness comes from the seed so the same line gives the same pauses and timing offline and live. Prebaked files and the live cache use the same key and the same output. Do the room (reverb) and EQ in Godot on the NPC audio bus (AudioEffectReverb or a send, EQ on the voice bus), not baked into files: then the same file sounds right indoors and out, and pre-baked and live lines match automatically. Live latency: phrase-level synthesis lets the first phrase play while the rest is made, and the pauses cost no synthesis time (just silence).
## 7 Is a newer or alternative model clearly better for the cost?
- Chatterbox (MIT, about 0.5B params, about 1 GB, English plus multilingual, voice cloning from a ~5 s clip, an "exaggeration" dial 0.0 to 1.0+ for emotion): clearly more expressive, but roughly 6x the size and GPU recommended: too heavy for live client-side voice on weak PCs; reasonable for PRE-BAKED key NPC lines (generated offline on a GPU, Dave's PC or a rented one).
- Coqui XTTS v2: non-commercial licence, not allowed.
- F5-TTS: weights are non-commercial (CC-BY-NC) as far as I know: check before any use.
- Dia (Apache-2.0, ~1.6B, dialogue with non-verbal tags like (laughs)) and Orpheus (Apache-2.0, 3B, emotion tags): expressive but GPU-heavy; verify licences and speed before testing.
- Kitten TTS: tiny and fast but lower quality than Kokoro.
Recommendation: stay on Kokoro for live voice (size, speed, licence), apply sections 1 to 6 first (an afternoon of work, likely most of the gain), then A/B 10 lines with Dave: Kokoro with the new pauses versus Chatterbox pre-baked. If Dave prefers Chatterbox, use it only for pre-baked story NPCs and keep Kokoro as the live voice with the same voice settings where possible (a voice mismatch between live and baked lines of the same NPC would be worse than either alone, so use the same model per NPC).

# Report 25 (2026-10-21): random client ground blocks arrive as sdf 0.00 (a solid box) where the server has air. Usual causes in a streamed voxel pipeline, and the standard guards. Read from src/stream/network_voxel_stream.gd, client_block_cache.gd and shared/baked_block_codec.gd; the cause itself is NOT found, these are ranked suspects with a check each.
Practice reference: Minecraft region files (per-chunk data version, CRC or length check, write to temp file then rename), Luanti/Minetest map blocks (format version byte, zlib body, timestamp, dirty flag, resend modified blocks only), Voxel Tools (VoxelStreamSQLite with a block format version, deserialize reports failure), Vintage Story and Space Engineers (versioned chunk saves, per-chunk revision).
## Suspects, most likely first
1 UNIFORM SDF 0.0 IS IMPOSSIBLE BY CONSTRUCTION, SO IT IS ALWAYS A DECODE OR DEFAULT BUG. A signed distance block that is completely uniform must lie at or beyond the clamp (|sdf| at least one voxel, in practice the max clamp): a uniform 0.0 means the channel was never written and has the buffer default (raw 0). Typical sources: a uniform or compact encoding where "no data" and "value 0" are the same byte; an empty or compact payload (like the 0xA1 deep-granite marker) decoded to the wrong channel; a deserialize that returned false but left the pooled out_buffer unchanged; a worker thread reading the buffer before the write finished. Guard: after any decode, reject a block whose SDF channel is uniform with |value| < clamp (treat as corrupt: do not cache, ask again).
2 THE UNPACK CHECK ONLY WATCHES ONE CHANNEL. _store plants a marker in CHANNEL_RESOURCE and checks that channel after VoxelBlockSerializer.deserialize_from_byte_array; the call's return value is not used and the SDF channel is not checked. A partial unpack that rewrote resource but not SDF (or vice versa) passes. Guard: use the return value, plant markers in every used channel (SDF, type/geology, deposit, resource), or deserialize into a fresh VoxelBuffer and copy, never into a reused one; and verify the SDF uniform rule above.
3 NO CHECK ON THE DISK CACHE READ. The checksum (hash of the body, last 4 bytes) is verified only on blocks arriving from the network; cache.load_block returns whatever the SQLite file holds with no revalidation, and the starter cache (seed_starter, gzipped sqlite files built "by running a client at the spawn") is copied in blind. A bad block written once (during a session with another bug, a crash, a re-bake, or when two clients or tasks write the same file) stays forever and looks random. Guard: store a per-block checksum and block format version with every cached block and verify on read (or run a one-time scan of the cache: count uniform SDF 0.0 blocks); the already-shipped hint "Clear saved ground" fixing it is itself a sign the cache holds it. Test: clear the cache and play the same route: if the boxes vanish, the cache is the carrier; if they come back, the network or decode is.
4 REVISION KEY IS PER TILE ONLY. Cache files are tile_x_z_r<revision>.sqlite. Ground changes that do not raise the tile revision (saved ground edits applied on the server, starter-cache rebuilt for a different build, TERRAIN_VERSION bumps within one session, water or river carve changes) leave old blocks in the cache. Guard: include a content version (terrain version + edits revision + codec FORMAT_VERSION = 2) in the key or per block, and have the server resend dirty blocks (version stamp per block, client sends its stamp, server replies "unchanged" or the block); this is what Luanti and Minecraft do.
5 PREFETCH AND SPARE RACES. Prefetch answers wait in _spare keyed by block; forget_prefetch only clears on a tile change; an answer that arrives after a timeout (30 s) or after a reset lands in _spare and may be used later against a newer state. The stream closed check protects the cache write but not _spare. Guard: tag every request and answer with the stream generation and tile revision; drop answers whose tag is old.
6 PARTIAL WRITE / THREADING. Voxel Tools worker threads call load and save at once; one SQLite stream per tile shared across threads and flushed on the main thread. A kill during flush, a full disk, or an antivirus lock on the file can leave a block half-written (SQLite is transactional, but the app-level multi-record updates are not). Guard: write blocks inside one transaction per flush, check free space, wrap in try and mark the tile cache "dirty" in a sidecar file that is cleared on a clean close; on next start, if dirty, discard that tile's file.
7 LOD AND NEIGHBOUR MISMATCH. Coarser LODs downsample SDF; if an LOD1+ block is computed or cached from a stale LOD0 or with a different clamp (SDF_BASE_MAX_DISTANCE 64, SDF_STORAGE_MAX_DISTANCE 500 in the codec; the clamp depends on LOD through _get_sdf_max_distance) a client with a different max distance reads the wrong scale, turning air to near-zero values. Guard: put the clamp distance and format version in the payload header and assert equal to the receiver's.
8 THE SERVER SIDE: check server_block_store.gd for a block built while the generator or ground_ops was mid-update (a ground edit not yet applied) and for blocks sent from a cache that ended up with uniform 0.0; apply the same uniform-SDF-0 invariant on send (assert and log with block coordinates).
## Standard guards (add all, in this order)
A Invariant check on every decoded and every cached block (uniform SDF must be beyond the clamp; non-uniform SDF within the clamp; channel lengths correct); a bad block is logged with block key, LOD, source (network, spare, disk), never cached, re-requested.
B Per-block version + checksum in the stored record and the wire payload; verify on read as well as arrival.
C Never decode into a reused buffer, check every channel, use the return value.
D Atomic, transactional cache writes and a dirty-flag file; full cache wipe on codec FORMAT_VERSION or TERRAIN_VERSION change.
E Per-block (or per-region) dirty versions from the server, with "unchanged" replies, so edits and rebakes refresh only what changed.
F A debug overlay or console command that lists the last 50 blocks that failed the invariant (key, LOD, source, bytes): one run in the box area will show whether network, spare or disk is the source. Also add the counter bad_blocks to the probe table.
G Reproduce quickly: log, for each block that becomes a solid box, its source (spare / disk / network) and cache age; the source tells you which of suspects 3, 5 or 1 it is.

# Report 26 (2026-10-21): the first-person axe chop looks wrong (Dave): how a real chop moves, how FPS games animate it, a keyframe table, and why ours is off. ## 1 How a real chop looks (felling or splitting with an axe; references: woodcutting form guides, biomechanics of axe swing, game viewmodel practice)
Phases: (a) anticipation / backswing: the axe is lifted up and back, elbows bend, wrist cocks so the head tilts behind the hands, weight goes back slightly, eyes stay on the cut; (b) a brief pause at the top (20-40 ms); (c) downswing: shoulders and hips rotate forward and the arms accelerate in an arc, the wrist uncocks late (the flick) so the head is fastest at the end; (d) impact at roughly chest height or below, hands in front of the body, arms nearly straight, blade at about 45 degrees to the trunk (for felling, alternating upper and lower cuts), the head stops dead; (e) follow-through is short, then recovery by pulling the axe out of the wood (a tug back) to the next backswing.
The path is mostly VERTICAL in the plane facing the tree: over the shoulder and down in front, with a 15-25 degree diagonal (right-handed: top right to centre-left). It is not a horizontal sweep (that is a bat or scythe).
Two-hand grip (felling axe): off-hand (left for a right-hander) at the butt end, dominant hand (right) higher; on the downswing the right hand slides down the shaft towards the left, ending close to it at impact. One-handed hatchet: grip stays put, more wrist, much less shoulder.
Timing for a 0.55 s chop (normal pace): backswing 40% (0.22 s) eased out, pause 5% (0.03 s), downswing 18% (0.10 s) eased in (fast), impact + hit stop 8% (0.04 s), recovery 29% (0.16 s). Hit damage, sound, particles on the impact frame (0.35 s), not before.
## 2 How good first-person games do it (Skyrim, Valheim, Rust, Minecraft-like viewmodels)
A dedicated viewmodel animation, authored in camera space (positions relative to the camera, hands and tool only, usually the lower right of the screen), separate from the third-person body animation; the camera never turns with the body twist; hands stay mostly in view during the wind-up (they rise into the top-right corner, the tool head visible, they do not go behind the camera); small camera motion supports it: a 1-2 degree pitch up in the backswing and a 2-3 degree dip with a short shake on impact; hit stop (freeze 40-60 ms) and a 2-3 cm recoil of the tool sell the weight; left hand only appears when it holds something (Dave's rule): either grips the shaft (two-hand tools) or is out of view.
