# New Generator Plan

Working plan for the new world generator described in `DESIGN_IDEAS.md` (Sections 4.1 to 4.11) and `WORLD_FORMAT_SPEC.md`. Each step ends with something that can be seen or measured before the next step starts. Work happens on branch `claude/generator-v2`; `main` stays untouched until the new generator replaces the old one.

## Ground rules

- New code lives in its own folder, `src/worldgen/`. The old volumetric pipeline stays untouched and working until the new one replaces it, then it is retired in one step.
- Every step is tested in headless Godot 4.7 before it is shown. Timings are recorded.
- Permanent formats follow `WORLD_FORMAT_SPEC.md`. Any change to them updates the spec first.
- Reuse what already works: tile coordinates, region mapping and wrapping, the resumable atomic write pattern, the 2D hydrology (ocean mask, flow, lakes, channels) and the SHA-256 seeds.

- Step 11 (geology and ore) first version: `src/worldgen/tile/tile_geology.gd`. Provinces are world-grid cells (about 3.8 km) whose kind comes from the sketch feature under them; each has a repeating rock layer stack starting just above its highest ground, folds or a basin sag, and its own features (granite plutons and stocks, faults, volcanic vents and pipes, salt domes) and ore bodies (tin roofs, copper halos, skarn iron/copper/zinc, gold and silver veins on faults, lead and zinc lenses in limestone, coal seams, salt and gypsum layers, gem pipes and pegmatites, sulfur around vents, placer gold and tin in rivers below exposed bodies). Maps: province index, cover depth, layer offset, plus a bedrock-surface rock map for previews. About 10 s per tile. Preview: `tile_3_4_geology.png`. Rules in `tile_rules.json` "geology". Known gaps: ore amounts are rough estimates, coal tonnes count one cycle only, the world wrap seam is not yet handled by the per-tile detail and climate noise (provinces do handle it).
- Step 12 (sites) first version: `src/worldgen/tile/tile_sites.gd`. Natural caves only (mineshafts and labyrinths are later add-ons): limestone caves that follow the folded limestone beds, with squeezes (often followed by a drop), shafts, ledges, chambers, a rare large cavern, chasms crossed by thin rock bridges and shallow flooded sumps; about half start near ore and send a side passage into it; lava tubes from volcanic vents on land, with skylights; sea caves in coastal cliffs; rock shelters. Each cave is a skeleton of nodes and typed segments plus chambers, entrances and creature dens (tier from regional danger and depth). Caves stay inside their tile. Also dormant lair sites (caves with a chamber and a den), the harbor, and on starter tiles the spawn point (beach beside the starter river mouth) and a suggested town block; no cave opens within 600 m of either. Under 1 s per tile. Preview: `tile_3_4_caves.png`. Rules in `tile_rules.json` "sites".
- Step 13 done (2026-10-15): `src/worldgen/tile/tile_file.gd` writes and reads the tile file, and `build_tile.gd --tile=3,4 --write` bakes the starter tile end to end and checks every section on read-back. **Checkpoint result:** whole tile in 137.8 s (generator about 2 minutes plus 1.6 s packing and 0.4 s compress-and-write), file 9.85 MB, read back and checksum-verified in 0.25 s. 831 checks pass. Tile files are git-ignored (`worlds/*/tiles/`).
- Step 13b done (2026-10-16): world river network on the 160 m map, tiles built on a 640 m margin and cropped, guide-carved tile rivers, wrap-seamless detail and climate noise, and `edge_match.gd`. The network river curves now meander: every curve point is pushed sideways by a smooth random field of world position (three octaves of plane waves, 2400/800/260 m wavelength, 80/35/12 m push; `tile_rules.json` water "network_meander"). Each wave fits a whole number of times into the world, so neighbors, both wrap seams and river junctions agree. The long straight 45 degree runs are gone from tile (3,4). Tiles (3,4), (3,3), (4,4), (3,5) and both seam pairs rebaked: 7 edges compared, 0 hard failures, all river crossings matched. 839 tests pass.
- Step 14 first version (2026-10-16): `src/worldgen/runtime/terrain_generator.gd` (a `VoxelGeneratorScript`) turns baked tiles into voxels for any block and LOD, with `tile_terrain_data.gd` (one loaded tile, about 40 MB in memory) and `terrain_layout.gd` (channels: SDF 16 bit, rock, soil, resource 8 bit each). Surface: Catmull-Rom of the 8 m heights plus two octaves of detail noise (faded out on water, wrap-seamless); the SDF is written voxel by voxel only in a thin band around the surface and filled in runs elsewhere. Rock: province layer stacks with the layer offset, cover as sediment with its soil id, granite intrusions with marble aureoles, basalt vents and pipes (the same rules as the baked surface-rock map; 90 percent or more agree just under the bedrock surface, the rest is the detail noise moving layer boundaries). Ore: lens, halo, pipe, vein, layer, scatter and placer, at LOD 0. Caves: passages and chambers carved out up to LOD 2. Rules in `tile_rules.json` "runtime_terrain". Tiles with no file are a flat sea floor at -200 m. Heights join across tile edges and the wrap seam (largest jump 0.001 m). 853 tests pass.
  - **Speed** (`tools/terrain_bench.gd`, 4-core cloud machine, one thread): a 32 m block at LOD 0 takes about 18 ms at the surface, 16 ms deep underground, 2 ms in open air; coarser LODs 16 to 30 ms. Four threads: 125 surface blocks per second. Voxel Tools' graph generator does the surface alone (no rock, ore or caves) in about 4 ms. The preview scene fills a 2 km view at the starter spawn in about 6 s.
  - **Preview:** `scenes/terrain_preview/terrain_preview.tscn` (or `--role=terrain`): fly or walk (G) over a baked tile, with a HUD showing the rock and soil under foot. `--frames=N` runs it headless as a check. Cross-sections: `plan/tile_3_4_section_peak.png`, `plan/tile_3_4_section_cave.png`.
  - **Choice of generator:** the graph generator cannot read the tile lists (rock stacks, ore bodies, caves), so it could only do the surface. GDScript does everything and is fast enough for one player and the preview. Recommendation: keep it until server streaming (step 16) shows it is too slow, then move the same code into a small C++ module compiled into the engine (the engine is already a custom build); expect about 20 to 50 times faster.
  - **Checkpoint:** measured in the cloud (load time and block speed above). Frame rate on the owner's machine waits for the first playtest: he does nothing on his own computer until the game is playable (2026-10-16).
  - **Known gaps:** rivers and lakes are not drawn (only a flat sea at 0 m); no rendering materials from the rock channel yet; faults do not shift layers and salt domes have no salt rock (as in the baked preview); heights are only C0 at tile edges (the interpolation does not read the neighbor tile).

## Steps

### Preparation

1. **Done (2026-10-16).** **Voxel Tools build.** Built with `tools/build_godot_linux.sh` (headless Linux, 27 minutes on 4 cores; reports `4.7.2.stable.custom_build.ed1daf0bf`). The project uses Voxel Tools compiled into Godot (there is no addon folder), so terrain steps need a matching Linux build in Claude's workspace. The project owner uses Godot 4.7.2 stable (custom build `ed1daf0bf`) with Voxel Tools 1.7. Get or compile a matching Linux build (Godot 4.7.2 source plus the Voxel Tools 1.7 module) before step 14.
2. **Folder layout and branch.** Create `src/worldgen/` with subfolders for settings, tables, matrix, tile pipeline, tile file and runtime. Create branch `claude/generator-v2`.

### Planning the world (no terrain yet)

3. **World settings.** `worlds/<world>/world.json` with identity, geometry, climate curve and runtime-tunable values, plus a loader that validates it. The climate function (latitude, temperature, altitude) lives here.
4. **Data tables.** Append-only ID tables for biomes, soils, rocks, resources, water classes, site types and feature types.
5. **Tile plan.** Originally a per-tile randomizer; replaced on 2026-10-13 by the world sketch and a tile plan derived from it (`plan/tile_plan.json`).
6. **Whole-world preview.** One image of all tiles (islands, climate zones, starter tiles) and a text summary (land per zone, islands per size class). **Checkpoint:** the project owner reviews and tunes the odds.

### Building one tile (2D maps)

7. **Island layout and elevation.** From the world elevation map: cubic upsample to 8 m plus seam-safe detail noise. Neighboring tiles match on shared edges. Preview image.
8. **Water.** Adapt the existing hydrology to the new height field: rivers, lakes, flow. Preview image.
9. **Erosion.** A 2D erosion pass for valleys and winding terrain. Timed.
10. **Climate, biome and soil.** Temperature, moisture, rain shadow, biome, soil, fertility. Preview images.
11. **Geology and ore.** Provinces, features (intrusions, faults, basins, volcanic), ore bodies with stable IDs, layer offsets and cover depth.
12. **Sites.** Natural cave systems, dormant lairs, harbors, and starter data for starter tiles. Mineshafts and labyrinths are later add-ons (decided 2026-10-13).
12b. **Done (2026-10-14).** **Add tuff to the geology.** Rock ID 11, with a 40 to 200 m tuff layer above the basalt in the volcanic province stacks. Rules are in `tile_rules.json` and covered by tests. Done before any tile is kept.
13. **Done (2026-10-15).** **Tile file.** Writer and reader for the container in the spec (sections, Zstandard, SHA-256, atomic rename), plus low-resolution maps. **Checkpoint:** bake a starter tile end to end, record the time and file size.
13b. **Done (2026-10-16).** **Rivers across edges and edge matching.** A world river network, tile rivers that follow it, overlap margins, wrap-seamless detail and climate noise, and a pass that checks every shared tile edge (spec Section 4b). Must be done before any tile is kept.

### Seeing it in the game

14. **First version done (2026-10-16); frame rate on the owner's machine waits for the playtest.** **Runtime terrain generator.** Turn the maps and lists into 1 m voxels: interpolation, detail noise, rock layers, caves, ore. Compare Voxel Tools' graph generator, a GDScript generator and, if needed, a small C++ one. **Checkpoint:** walk the tile in Godot and measure generation speed.
15. **First version done (2026-10-16); how it looks waits for the playtest.** **Flora at runtime.** Trees and plants placed from biome, moisture and the flora seed.
  - Species: `data/tables/flora.json` (append-only IDs): 11 trees, 7 shrubs, 9 plants, each with plants per hectare by biome, moisture range, steepest slope, altitude range, water classes it can stand in, height, crown and trunk sizes, color and yield names for harvesting later. Settings in `tile_rules.json` "runtime_flora".
  - Placement: `src/worldgen/runtime/flora_placer.gd`, per 256 m chunk. Each species has its own jittered grid and random stream (flora seed, chunk, species), so adding species or cutting trees never moves anything else; item id = species << 16 | grid cell, the stable "item N in chunk C" a removal records. Trees and shrubs keep their crowns apart; a world-wide noise field thins trees into glades where shrubs grow thicker. About 33 ms and 1,600 items per chunk on one cloud core.
  - Server: `src/stream/server_flora.gd` sends chunks (about 20 KB each, packed and compressed) and stores removals in `flora_removed.json`; flora requests run on the block workers after urgent blocks. Messages: request flora, deliver flora, cut flora (test harvest), flora cut (protocol 3).
  - Drawing: `src/flora/flora_view.gd` in the client and the preview: stand-in meshes (trunk plus crown by shape), one MultiMesh per species and part of a chunk; trees drawn to 900 m, shrubs 300 m, plants 120 m; trunk colliders within 160 m.
  - Measured (client and server headless on one machine): 52 chunks around the spawn in 1.6 s, 1.1 MB, 88,000 items held, 18,000 drawn. `src/worldgen/tools/flora_map.gd` draws `plan/tile_3_4_flora.png` (1 km around the spawn).
  - Known gaps: no regrowth yet; flora does not follow terrain edits; grass (client-only, Section 4.3) not drawn; real models, wind and frame rate wait for the playtest.
16. **First version done (2026-10-16).** **Server streaming and client cache.** Server-generated blocks streamed to clients, the generated block cache, the edits store and the client disk cache with a size limit.
  - Server: `scenes/terrain_server/` (headless, ENet port 24600, worker threads) with `src/stream/server_block_store.gd`. A block (16 voxels a side, any LOD) comes from the edits store, else the generated-block cache, else the generator (then cached). Both stores are Voxel Tools SQLite files in `user://server_store/<world>/`. Ore buried more than 2 m (times the LOD scale) below any surface is removed from the copy sent out. Each tile has a revision in `revisions.json` that rises when one of its blocks is edited.
  - Client: `scenes/terrain_client/` with `network_voxel_stream.gd` (a `VoxelStreamScript`) and `client_block_cache.gd`: one SQLite file per tile and revision in `user://terrain_cache/<world>/`; a newer revision deletes the tile's old file; over the size limit (default 2 GB) the farthest tiles go first, never the player's tile or its neighbors. Each block the terrain asks for that is not cached is requested as urgent along with its 26 neighbors as prefetch; the server makes urgent blocks first.
  - Messages (`src/stream/terrain_net.gd`, protocol 2, now 3 with flora): welcome, request blocks, deliver blocks (batched up to 192 KB), tile changed, dig (test edit) and dug (applied to the loaded terrain at once).
  - Measured headless on the 4-core cloud machine, server and client on one machine, 2 km view (7 LODs) at the starter spawn: 11,112 blocks. Cold (nothing cached anywhere): 33 s, 7.0 MB sent, about 435 bytes a block (prefetch adds about 50 percent). Server cache warm, client cache empty: 25 s. Client cache warm: 0.4 s, nothing sent. Client cache 12.8 MB. A 4 m dig: the tile's revision rose, the client carved the hole at once, the next client run got the dug blocks from the edits store.
  - Known gaps: an edit invalidates the whole tile in client caches (per-region revisions later); lower LODs are not rebuilt after an edit; no floating origin yet; frame rate on the minimum machine is step 17.
17. **Minimum machine test.** The project owner runs the client on the older quad-core and reports frame rate and memory.

### Finishing

18. **Done for the inner set (2026-10-16).** **Bake the inner starter tiles** and their neighborhoods.
  - Baked the four inner starters (-3,4), (3,4), (-3,-4), (3,-4) and their eight neighbors each: 36 tiles, 21 to 41 s each, 4 to 9 MB each, about 12 minutes running three at a time. The starter tile (3,4) came out byte-for-byte the same as the committed copy.
  - The files are kept in the project folder, `/mnt/project-files/tiles/base_world/` (213 MB, not in git). Copy them into `worlds/base_world/tiles/` to use them.
  - Edge check over the 59 shared edges: heights, water, provinces, layer offsets and river crossings all match. Two small soft mismatches remain: flow inside a lake that straddles (-4,3)/(-4,4) (23 samples; each tile sees a different share of the lake's catchment), and heath against temperate forest on 12 samples at the north end of (3,-5)/(4,-5) (the "heath within 600 m of the sea" rule near a tile corner). Neither shows in the terrain; fix them when the bake code is next touched.
19. **Retire the old pipeline** and update `README.md`, `GENERATION_GUIDE.md` and `PROJECT_ROADMAP.md`.
